## 1. Scope — what E7 is, in the owner's terms

Doc 10 §1 is a **solar-system industrial warfare sandbox** where every battle has an economic cause. Doc 10 §11 names the first playable corridor — **Mars–Phobos–Ceres** — and its proving story: *extract water and metals near Ceres → hire players to haul to Phobos → fight or evade an interception → manufacture or refit a ground unit → load it aboard a carrier → deploy to Mars → complete a ground operation that changes control or access → salvage, repair, pay, repeat.* **Q22A** bounds E7 to **one coherent narrow vertical slice**: that loop end to end, with **one ground engagement and one local-space interception**, both fought with **authored** units from the rules pack. E7 builds:

- **A world made of packs, not code** (Q6A/Q18A) — immutable digest-referenced rules packs and once-applied campaign-seed packs; only what a table cannot express is Rust.
- **Quantity truth with a location** (Q2D/Q13A) — a neutral `mush-inventory` owning location-bound pools *and* lineage-bearing lots, every balance on an **E3 custody endpoint**, never a locationless account balance.
- **Industry that plans legibly** (Q1A/Q14A/Q3A) — deposits with deterministic curves and revealed *bands*; `ClaimRecord` tenure with licences as entitlements; facilities, recipes and work orders reserving matter, money and capacity through a **C8 shape-conformant** record.
- **One character, one active station** (C2A/C4A/C5A/Q20A) — billets stay many; the live control surface is the one focused station's K5a `ChannelGrant`, never a union.
- **Voyages as inhabited play** (C16A/C20A/Q15A) — the carrier root travels with its interiors, occupants, cargo and carried craft coherent; an interception **promotes** it into a live domain and demotes it at a quiet boundary with a durable summary.
- **One propagation layer** (Q4A/Q16A/R4) — detection and delivery over one physical context; contacts belong to a platform, never a side; orders are envelopes that must actually arrive.
- **A deterministic pulse** (S2A/Q12A/T2) — `DomainClock::Pulse`, tactical ingress on the prepared K lane, **one commit per domain per tick**, and no economic transaction inside a pulse.
- **Declared operations that settle** (Q5D/T6/Q9A/Q17A) — frozen terms plus mutable runtime under one `OperationId`, formations as constraint sets bound to org scopes, a bond as an economy hold, one journaled resolution root fact with idempotent consumers.
- **Crew and seats** (C11A/C15A/T10–T16/Q10D) — named decision NPCs plus an aggregate complement, readiness on the operation cadence, **consent-grant** proxy seats with dual attribution and deterministic fallback.
- **A floor under loss** (S3A/Q7A/T7) — a funded recovery organization leasing loaners against an eligibility test over *accessible* assets, never a faucet.

**Exit criterion.** Doc 10 §11's proving loop runs end to end on the seed pack, with the ground engagement and the local-space interception both resolving through the pulse driver and settling through `campaign.operation.resolved`.

### 2.3 K3 audit closures E7 may assume *(K)*

- **K3.1a — checkpoint proof (closed):** memory and Redis atomically verify every exact replay post-image **before** deleting the full intent; a mismatch retains the intent and fails closed (`09:2057-2058`).
- **K3.1b — store-owned admission (closed):** the store refuses a second unresolved authoritative reservation, so two pending bundles cannot claim the same fresh object id (`09:2059-2061`).
- Bundle hard caps — `crates/mush-core/src/authoritative.rs:27-38` *(main)*: replay writes 1_024 (`:27-28`), definitions 256 (`:29-30`), witnesses 1_024 (`:31-32`), durable-outbox intents 256 (`:33-34`), payload 256 KiB (`:35-36`), bundle 16 MiB (`:37-38`). Q12A and 10a §6 sharpening 4 call these "the real cap on a domain's active unit count"; **that is true of the tick commit, but only three of the six can bind it** — `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_REPLAY_WRITES` (1_024) bounds *how many* per-unit post-images a tick may write, `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_BUNDLE_BYTES` (16 MiB) and `MAX_DURABLE_OUTBOX_PAYLOAD_BYTES` (256 KiB) bound *how large* they may be. The other three are irrelevant to a tick. Cut 8 states which one binds, and §9 q6 measures it. **`MAX_DURABLE_OUTBOX_INTENTS` (256) is not the accepted-intent cap** — outbox intents are what a bundle emits, not what a tick consumes; Draft 0.1 derived `MAX_INTENTS_PER_TICK` from it, which was a category error (§7).
- **K3.1e — Redis-gated coverage (closed):** Redis tests are explicit ignored tests locally **and** an explicit Linux CI job, so skipped Lua coverage cannot count as passing (`09:2071-2078`).

### 2.4 The three kernel-migration gates, and the K6 control gate

Before **any** production reservation or settlement moves off the economy journal onto authoritative K3 state families, all three must land (proposal §2.3; 08c §12's K3 review, `08c:1793-1813` *(main)*, the three gates named at `:1804-1805`): (1) an authoritative bundle can carry the **mixed owner write set**, including the Ledger append; (2) ordinary module code can **synchronously read its typed state families outside an action**; (3) the **Ledger seal is folded into the same exact bundle** rather than acting as an external predecessor barrier.

Then a **migration review** opens. Q3A adopts 08c §12 verbatim: it "is then still an explicit **owner decision**, not a mechanical port" (`08c:1806-1808`; `:1810-1813` is the separate E0-hazard note) — **the trigger opens a review, never an automatic migration**. §5 states what would move.

**Separately, K6 gates control handoff.** K5a grants "establish eligibility and stale-intent witnesses; they are not active exclusive leases" (proposal §2.4; `09:2151` *(K)*). 10a **Q10/Q20** therefore let cut 10 ship seat **grants**, fallback and dual attribution while the **live handoff is stubbed behind the K6 gate**: E7 may assign title, maintenance and custody, but must not silently evict a live operator or transfer an active movement/weapons lease.

### 3.1 Cut 1 — `ruleset-tacsim` skeleton and the corridor fixture

**Records** (Q6A: everything expressible as a table is a versioned TOML content pack, in two kinds).

```rust
enum PackKind { RulesDefinition, CampaignSeed }
struct PackManifest { id: CanonicalId, version: u32, kind: PackKind, digest: DefinitionDigest,
                      requires: Vec<(CanonicalId, u32)> }
struct RulesPack { manifest: PackManifest, entries: BTreeMap<CanonicalId, PackEntry> }
struct RouteDef { id: CanonicalId, endpoints: (LocationId, LocationId), legs: Vec<LegDef>,   // ← G1
                  alignment_band: CadenceId, drive_class: CanonicalId, propellant: u64 }
struct SeedApplication { pack: CanonicalId, version: u32, digest: DefinitionDigest,
                         applied_at: ClockStamp, objects: u32 }          // `tacsim:seed:{pack}`
```

A **rules-definition pack** is immutable and referenced by digest: propulsion and route tables, the ten commodity families (S4A), component definitions, recipes, formation kinds (Q9A), combat and insurance policy. A **campaign-seed pack** instantiates mutable world state **once**: corridor locations, organizations, deposits, facilities, inventories, NPCs. The distinction is load-bearing — *a rules pack is re-pinned and diffed, while re-running a seed pack would duplicate a mine* (Q6A). `[tacsim.*]` host config carries deployment switches and pack paths only, mirroring `[shadowrun.economy].catalog_paths` (08f §9).

**`RouteDef` closes G1.** There is no route type in the tree: a journey's route is only `<room>[:secs]` tokens parsed in `route_create` (`crates/mush-sim/src/commands.rs:1584-1648`, main) over `Leg { to, travel_s }` (`route.rs:45-52`). T1 needs a *definition* — route cost and duration must be deterministic functions of the route definition, drive and payload, declared departure, propellant plan, and the body-pair alignment band on a campaign cadence — so the definition is pack data from cut 1 and cut 6 consumes it.

**Invariants.** (i) A published digest is never mutated; a change publishes a **new version that coexists** (Q18A), and live units, blueprints, recipes and open contracts stay **pinned** until an explicit migration or in-world refit — T1's guarantee that a published hauling contract's route assumptions cannot shift underneath it. (ii) A seed pack applies at most once per `(pack, version)`; the `SeedApplication` document is the idempotency latch, written in the same batch as the objects. (iii) Loading is deterministic — same bytes, same digest, same iteration order. (iv) An unknown, oversized or digest-mismatched pack **fails closed at boot** and names the file. The engine already enforces the shape: `DomainRecord.driver` is an exact digest-bearing `DefinitionRef` (`crates/mush-sim/src/data.rs:227-232`, main) and K3 validates exact descriptors at registration (`crates/mush-core/src/module.rs:622-634`, main).

**The tiny corridor fixture.** Three locations — a Ceres extraction site, a transfer node, a Phobos yard; two commodities — `metals` and `structural_alloys`, both from S4A's ten; one recipe — `alloy_smelt@1`; one `RouteDef` joining Ceres to Phobos. Enough for cuts 2–4 to run and for the owner to see output first.

**Commands.** `@tacsim/pack list|show <id>|verify` · `@tacsim/seed <pack> [dry|commit]` (staff) · `@tacsim/pin <subject>=<pack>@<version>` (staff, for a migration or refit).

**Tests (~22).** *Digest:* identical bytes → identical digest; a whitespace-only edit changes it; a reordered table does not (canonical encoding); `MAX_PACK_ENTRIES` refuses. *Load:* two loads are byte-identical in memory; an unknown key, a missing `requires` dependency and a `requires` version mismatch each fail closed naming the file. *Seed:* `dry` writes nothing and lists every planned object; `commit` applies once; a second `commit` is a HIT writing nothing; a *different version* of the same pack is refused with the applied version named; a crash between object writes and the latch converges on reboot. *Pinning:* a subject pinned to `v1` is unaffected by publishing `v2`; both versions load concurrently; an unpinned consumer of a retired version fails closed rather than silently upgrading. *Routes:* a `RouteDef`'s duration is a pure function of definition, drive and payload; two evaluations agree; an unknown drive class refuses. *Fixture:* the three locations, two commodities, recipe and route exist after `commit` and nowhere else; the fixture is reproducible from an empty store; boot with a corrupt pack refuses to start. *Added by the review:* **two `commit`s racing** (two processes, one store) leave exactly one applied set and one latch, and the loser is a HIT, not a partial write; a pack whose bytes parse but whose declared digest was computed over **non-canonical** bytes is refused at load, not at first use; the digest covers the canonical encoding and **not** the manifest's own `digest` field (a self-referential hash is unverifiable); every `PackManifest`, `RulesPack` and `SeedApplication` **re-enters through its constructor on load** (`TryFrom` wire, §4) and a stored pack over `MAX_PACK_ENTRIES` is refused rather than truncated; `@tacsim/pack show` on an unknown id and on a **known but unreadable** id answer identically (no pack-existence oracle for a non-staff viewer); `@tacsim/seed` on a store already carrying a *different* pack's objects applies only its own and names nothing about theirs.

**Verify lenses.** **P (pack identity)** — can two byte streams produce one digest, or one stream two? **S (seed idempotency)** — can any interleaving or crash duplicate a seeded object or lose the latch? **B (boot fail-closed)** — does any malformed pack degrade silently instead of refusing?

**Hands to cut 2:** the commodity family list, `alloy_smelt@1`, the `RouteDef` type, and the digest discipline every later definition reference uses.

### 3.2 Cut 2 — `mush-inventory`, quantity truth with a location

**Ov, stated.** The proposal §4 gives `WorkOrder` a `material_source: Option<AssetContainerRef>` and speaks of a "material vault". **Q13A/Q2D override this**: quantities are neither asset containers nor account balances. A neutral `mush-inventory` is *the single source of quantity truth*, bound to **custody endpoints**, and industry, markets, contracts and simulation consume it — **none keeps a private stock number**. The proposal's material-vault quantities live here; `material_source` becomes an `EndpointRef`.

**Records** (Q2D fixes the fields exactly: **not** `legal`, **not** `custody`).

```rust
struct EndpointRef { provider: OwnerId, object: ObjId }     // an E3 custody endpoint
struct Pool  { endpoint: EndpointRef, commodity: CanonicalId, quantity: u64, revision: u64 }
struct Lot   { id: LotId, commodity: CanonicalId, quantity: u64, grade: Grade, lineage: Lineage }
enum  Lineage { Origin { source: ExternalRef }, Split { parent: LotId },
                Merge { parents: Vec<LotId> },              // ≤ MAX_LOT_PARENTS
                Tranche { tranche: TrancheId } }
struct PoolTranche { id: TrancheId, endpoint: EndpointRef, commodity: CanonicalId,
                     absorbed: Vec<LotId>, quantity: u64 }  // immutable lineage root
struct InventoryReservation {                               // doc 09 C8, exact fields (09:997-1006)
    id: ReservationId, owner: OwnerId, resource: ResourceRef, holder: ScopedIdempotencyKey,
    amount: u64, revision: u64, expires: ClockStamp,
    state: ReservationState,                                // Held | Consumed(commit) | Released | Expired
}
// ReservationId MUST render to a canonical `IdempotencyKeyText` (≤ MAX_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_BYTES
// = 128, `action.rs:31`, `:296-320`), because that is the only shape
// `AuthorityGuard::Reservation { provider, id, revision }` can name (`action.rs:1060-1064`).
```

**Custody is the endpoint's, not the lot's** (Q2D): a lot has one location and one custodian because its endpoint does. **Stolen or disputed status follows the *title* record** (`Encumbrance::Disputed`, `record.rs:521-527`, main; E6b's `report_stolen`). **Embargo legality is a jurisdiction/policy test at transaction time**, not a field travelling with matter — the same cargo is legal one hop away. **Licensing is the acting principal's entitlement**, not the cargo's (Q1A/Q14A).

**Verbs.** `credit`/`debit` a pool · `split(lot, n)` · `merge(lots)` · `transfer(what, to_endpoint)` · `reserve`/`consume(commit)`/`release`/`expire`. Interconversion is **one journaled, attributed act each way** (Q2D/T5): `dissolve(lot)` creates or updates an immutable `PoolTranche` and credits the pool; `crystallize(pool, n)` mints a lot whose **parent is that tranche**. **Lineage is never discarded — only coarsened in the active projection.**

**Invariants.** (1) Conservation under checked arithmetic; a `debit` below available (balance minus live holds) refuses. (2) Locality: no verb moves quantity except *between endpoints*; there is no endpoint-free balance. (3) The custody rules are E3's, unchanged — exactly one claiming provider (`custody.rs:103-131`), validate-then-mutate (`:195-226`); a hold that moves with a ship needs no second mechanism. (4) Lineage is append-only and acyclic, bounded by `MAX_LOT_PARENTS`/`MAX_LINEAGE_DEPTH`. (5) The reservation state machine is doc 09 C8's exactly; availability subtracts every live hold; the consuming action verifies holder **and** revision and applies the post-image in the same batch. (6) `mush-inventory` registers an `AuthorityGuardKind::Reservation` validator under its **own** `OwnerId`, through `ModuleRegistry::authority_guard_validator` (`mush-core/src/module.rs:1792-1800`, main); the registry is keyed `(OwnerId, AuthorityGuardKind)` (`action.rs:1420`, `:1520`) and routed per key (`:1534-1561`), so cut 4 registering the *same kind* under `mush-industry`'s `OwnerId` is legal by construction, not by luck. **There is no `brace` pattern to copy** (G3): `brace` registers definition validators, never an authority-guard validator, and no production module registers one. (7) Every record above uses the `TryFrom` wire (§4).

**Commands.** `@inv/show <endpoint>` · `@inv/lots <endpoint> [commodity]` · `@inv/lot <id>` (lineage, paged) · `@inv/move <what>=<endpoint>` · `@inv/split <lot>=<n>` · `@inv/merge <lot>,<lot>…` · `@inv/dissolve <lot>` · `@inv/crystallize <endpoint>/<commodity>=<n>` · `@inv/reservations <endpoint>`.

**Tests (~28).** *Conservation:* every verb sums; a `debit` past available refuses and writes nothing; `u64` overflow on merge refuses; a zero-quantity split refuses. *Lineage:* split → merge → split reconstructs both parents; `dissolve` then `crystallize` leaves the tranche as the child's parent and the original lot id reachable; a merge over `MAX_LOT_PARENTS` refuses; a cycle is unconstructible; coarsening the projection deletes no stored parent; `MAX_LINEAGE_DEPTH` refuses. *Locality:* no verb produces an endpoint-free pool; moving a ship moves its hold's contents by construction; a transfer to a destroyed endpoint refuses; a transfer between endpoints claimed by two providers refuses. *Reservations:* two orders cannot hold one unit; `consume` twice with the same commit is a HIT; a stale revision refuses; `release` after `consume` refuses; expiry is idempotent and survives restart; availability is balance minus live holds at every read. *Restart:* every record re-enters through its constructor on load (E5a lens-A: deserialization must not walk past the caps); a corrupt lot document is skipped **loudly** and its endpoint marked, never silently zeroed. *Custody:* a lot in a vault inherits the vault's access; `may_take` refuses a non-custodian. *Added by the review:* a **corrupt lot skipped loudly must not raise availability** — the endpoint is marked and every `debit`/`reserve` against it refuses until reconciled, because the fail-open shape here is "the missing lot was the one the hold was against" (E5a lens A); a `Consumed` reservation replayed with the **same commit token under a different holder** refuses rather than HITs; availability read *between* the two writes of a partially applied batch never exceeds the pre-image; a `ReservationId` that will not render as `IdempotencyKeyText` is refused at construction, not at guard time; `@inv/show` on an endpoint the viewer may not read is byte-identical to `@inv/show` on one that does not exist; a `transfer` whose source and destination are the same endpoint is a no-op HIT, not a double credit; a reservation whose `expires` stamp is on a **different clock generation** refuses comparison rather than expiring early (K4a `checked_cmp`).

**Verify lenses.** **C (conservation and lineage)** — can any sequence create, destroy or orphan quantity, or lose a parent? **L (locality)** — can a balance exist without a live endpoint, or move without a custody act? **R (reservation)** — can two holders spend one unit, or a `Consumed` hold be released?

**Hands to cut 3:** the endpoint-bound pool/lot API extraction writes into, and the C8-conformant reservation shape cut 4 reuses for capacity.

### 3.3 Cut 3 — `mush-industry`: deposits, claims, survey, extraction

**Records** (Q1A and Q14A, verbatim field lists). `Deposit` is `mush-industry`'s; **`mush-economy` already owns an unrelated `DepositRecord`** (`contract.rs:567`, escrow bookkeeping) and neither name may drift into the other crate.

```rust
struct Deposit { id: DepositId, region: RegionId, commodity: CanonicalId, grade: Grade,
                 accessibility: Accessibility, reserve: u64, curve: YieldCurve,
                 surveyed_bands: BTreeMap<PrincipalId, SurveyBands> }
struct SurveyBands { grade_class: BandId, reserve_class: BandId, at: ClockStamp, fact: ExternalRef }
struct ClaimRecord { id: ClaimId, deposit_or_area: ClaimSubject, issuer: PrincipalId,
                     holder: PrincipalId, exclusivity: Exclusivity, priority: u32, term: ClockStamp,
                     transferability: Transferability, contest_state: ContestState, revision: u64 }
enum ContestState { Uncontested, Contested { contract: ContractId }, Suspended { by: PrincipalId },
                    Seized { by: PrincipalId, at: ClockStamp } }
```

A **licence is not tenure**: it is an `InstrumentKind::Entitlement` account issued by a government principal (`record.rs:50-61`, main) with campaign-cadence expiry, proving **authorization** to survey, extract or export — checked **at transaction time** against the *acting principal* (Q1A/Q14A/Q2D).

**Yield is deterministic** (Q1A). `curve` maps cumulative extraction to per-cycle yield; depletion is arithmetic, not a roll. **Variation comes from discrete campaign events** — accessibility change, equipment condition, collapse, licence suspension — each an attributed journaled act moving effective yield, never a per-period roll. A **survey reveals bands, never the exact reserve**, and the revealed band is per principal and is itself a recorded fact.

**Extraction** is scheduled, journaled **lot creation** on a deterministic key — doc 09 C8 already lists production and upkeep among bounded-idempotency-key actions (`09:1019-1028`, main). One cycle produces one `Lot` with `Lineage::Origin` at the extractor's endpoint (cut 2) and debits `reserve` in the same batch.

**Tenure lifecycle** (Q14A). A claim is **transferable**, **pledgeable** as collateral (`Encumbrance::Collateral`, `record.rs:537-542`, main), **contestable** through an E4 contract with a `Before` deadline and a named adjudicator (T3's shape), **suspendable** and **seizable** by the issuing authority. Overlapping political authority needs no fiction that an entitlement balance is land title: two governments may each issue a claim over one area, and `ControlKind::{DeJure, DeFacto}` (`mush-faction/src/data.rs:414-430`, main) says which is worth anything on the ground.

**Owner abandonment lands here** (T3; doc 10 §15; 08f §1.1 keeps it "in E7 with the mechanical recipe written out"): a unilateral `AssetEffect` setting `title_owner` to none with custody unchanged, a **direct attributed asset action** that need not masquerade as a bilateral contract — next to the claim seizure that needs the same unilateral title effect. The salvage claim that turns possession into title stays an ordinary contract with a `Before` contest deadline and a named adjudicator.

**Commands.** `@deposit/list <region>` · `@deposit/show <id>` (bands only, per viewer) · `@survey <deposit>` · `@claim/file <subject>=<issuer>` · `@claim/show|transfer|pledge|release` · `@claim/contest <claim>` · `@claim/suspend|seize <claim>` (issuer) · `@extract/start <deposit>=<facility>` · `@asset/abandon <object>` · `@licence/show <principal>`.

**Tests (~26).** *Band leakage:* an unsurveyed principal sees no band; a surveyed one sees **only** their band and never `reserve`, `curve` or another principal's band; a render at every verbosity is asserted not to contain the exact reserve; staff is the only exact read; a transferred claim does not transfer the transferor's bands. *Depletion:* cumulative extraction matches the curve over 200 cycles; a depleted deposit yields zero and refuses a new cycle; concurrent extractors cannot over-draw (each cycle is one reserve-consume); an accessibility event changes the *next* cycle, never a completed one; overflow refuses. *Claims:* two claims over one deposit coexist with distinct issuers and priorities; an exclusive claim refuses a second from the same issuer; a contest opens exactly one contract and moves `contest_state`; a contest settling for the challenger transfers the claim once, idempotently; suspension blocks extraction while leaving the record; seizure is attributed; a pledged claim cannot be transferred while the encumbrance stands; a claim past `term` stops authorizing and does not vanish. *Licences:* extraction without the entitlement refuses; an expired licence refuses at transaction time though the claim stands; an org-held licence authorizes a member acting on the org's scope, not the member personally. *Abandonment:* title goes to none, custody is unchanged, the act is attributed, and a re-run is a HIT. *Restart:* an extraction cycle interrupted mid-batch produces either no lot or exactly one. *Added by the review:* **refusals are indistinguishable** — an extraction refused for depletion, for a missing licence and for a suspended claim answer one message to a principal who may not know the reserve, and only staff get the reason (08c §6.2's D1 shape); `@deposit/show` on an unknown id and on a known unsurveyed one are byte-identical; a **band boundary is not an oracle** — repeated surveys by one principal do not narrow the band, and two principals' bands are computed from the same recorded classes, never from `reserve` re-quantised per viewer; a `SurveyBands` row is written once per `(deposit, principal, at)` and a replay is a HIT; a claim contest whose adjudicator contract is itself `Disputed` **suspends the claim rather than resolving it**, and pages level 2 with the command filled in; `ContestState::Seized` on a **pledged** claim refuses and pages, because seizure would silently destroy a lender's collateral; every record re-enters through its constructor (§4), and a `surveyed_bands` map over `MAX_SURVEY_BANDS` is refused on load, not truncated.

**Verify lenses.** **F (fog)** — can a viewer learn a reserve, a curve or another principal's band through any render, error, or arithmetic difference? **D (depletion)** — can total extracted exceed the reserve under concurrency, restart or event ordering? **T (tenure)** — can a claim be transferred, extracted under, or contested by someone the authority chain does not permit?

**Hands to cut 4:** lots at a facility endpoint, and the licence check the work order re-runs.

### 3.4 Cut 4 — `mush-industry`: facilities, recipes, work orders

This cut **is** the production-slice proposal, folded onto the binding decisions. Its §4 shapes, §5 clock semantics, §7 scenario and §8 E7a–E7d gates are adopted; four things change.

**Three overrides and one specification.** (1) **Ov — materials come from `mush-inventory`, not an asset container or "material vault"** (Q2D/Q13A) — `material_source: EndpointRef`. (2) **Ov — the value reservation is an E4 `Hold`, not an industry reservation**: T4 and doc 10 §15 keep holds money-only and forbid a resource field, while the proposal lists money, materials and capacity as undifferentiated "reservations". (3) *Not an override, a specification* — **the capacity reservation is Q3A's C8 shape-conformant record in `mush-industry`, holder = the work order's deterministic key**, with a named migration **trigger that opens a review, never an automatic migration** (§5); the proposal already keeps the economy journal authoritative (§2.3), so this adds a shape rather than reversing one. (4) **Ov — the campaign, not the ruleset, registers a restamp policy** (Q8D); the proposal's §3 boundary gives the ruleset "clock choice", which Q8D reserves to the kernel (generations) and the campaign layer (rate policy), leaving the ruleset a pack value.

**Records** (proposal §4, amended).

```rust
struct RecipeRef  { owner: OwnerId, id: CanonicalId, version: u32, digest: DefinitionDigest }
struct FacilityRecord { object: ObjId, owner: PrincipalId, capabilities: BTreeSet<CanonicalId>,
                        capacity_revision: u64, policy_revision: u64,
                        active_slots: u32, maximum_slots: u32 }
struct WorkOrder { id: WorkOrderId, revision: u64, requester: PrincipalId, beneficiary: PrincipalId,
                   facility: ObjId, recipe: RecipeRef, quantity: u32,
                   budget_account: AccountId,             // money → an E4 Hold (T4)
                   material_source: Option<EndpointRef>,  // ← was AssetContainerRef (Q2D)
                   destination: DeliveryTarget, holds: Vec<HoldId>,
                   reservations: Vec<ReservationId>, schedule: Option<ProductionSchedule>,
                   state: WorkOrderState, visibility: EconomicVisibility }
struct ProductionSchedule { started: ClockStamp, due: ClockStamp, clock_policy: CanonicalId,
                            progress_units: u64, total_units: u64 }
enum WorkOrderState { Draft, AwaitingApproval { request: ApprovalRequestId }, Ready, Running,
                      Interrupted { cause: InterruptionCause }, AwaitingDelivery,
                      Completed { commit: CommitToken }, Cancelled, Failed }
```

`active_slots` is an **advisory counter, not authority** — the proposal's own checklist says so. Availability is derived from live capacity reservations at every read, exactly as `held_amount` is a re-derived projection in E4 (08c D-E4-1).

**What an accepted order holds** (proposal §4, re-typed): money/requisition value as an E4 `Hold`; each conserved material input as a cut-2 inventory reservation; one facility slot or measured capacity as an industry reservation; any unique source asset being repaired or refit as an E4 custody lock/`Encumbrance`; an optional bounded destination capacity claim as an inventory reservation on the destination endpoint. **Every one uses the work order's deterministic holder key.** Completion consumes all required holds in the same transition that makes output available; cancellation and rejection release idempotently. A failed delivery may keep a completed output in `AwaitingDelivery` but **may not re-run production or consume the inputs twice**.

**One context, two resolvers — not one reachability step** (Q4A). They share geometry, medium, range, occlusion, interference and time-of-flight, and answer different questions. **Detection** resolves signature, emissions posture, medium, line of sight, sensor capability, noise floor and observer posture. **Delivery** resolves addressing, transmitter/receiver compatibility, routing, relays, encryption, jamming and store-and-forward. **Contacts are local; sharing is a delivery** (Q16A). **Orders require delivery** (T15). Issuing revision *N* does not make it the recipient's active order. The recipient obeys the **newest valid revision it has actually received**; under ECM it continues its last order and fallback doctrine while the commander sees the replacement unacknowledged. On restored reachability, deliver the **newest valid superseding order**, not an obsolete backlog. **An acknowledgement is itself a delivery** that must reach the issuer (Q4A) — which is *why* the commander can see a replacement unacknowledged. Ordinary jamming blocks or delays; content corruption and deception require an explicit electronic-warfare result and are later.

**Cadence** (Q4A). Tactical propagation runs on the **domain tick** (cut 8). With no domain active, interplanetary mail, market reports and couriers run a **coarse scheduled delivery pass on a campaign cadence**.

### 3.8 Cut 8 — the minimal deterministic pulse driver

**Q12A, both halves.** **Ingress:** player commands and externally submitted tactical intents are **new authoritative state-family actions and take the prepared path**. `brace` proves *part* of the shape — typed state family (`crates/mush-tactics/src/module.rs:592-621`, main), early idempotency and checked revision (`:504-536`), authoritative-only preparation (`:544-581`), definition-validator + `action_provider` registration (`:623-635`). It proves **no guard revalidation**: `brace` contains no guard at all (G3, §2.5), so cut 8 writes that half without a pattern. 12C — a TacSim-private action path — is forbidden by doc 09 C2 and T2.

**What a tick's invocation actually is, because the K lane has no actorless entry.** `ActionInvocation` requires `{ request, principal: PrincipalId, actor: ObjId, offered_action: ActionOfferId, subject: PerceivedRef, targets, parameters, idempotency: ScopedIdempotencyKey }` (`crates/mush-core/src/action.rs:687-698`, main) and is itself `#[serde(try_from = "ActionInvocationWire")]` (`:686`). A domain tick has no player. It is therefore committed as one invocation with **`principal: PrincipalId::Scoped { owner, class, id }`** (`action.rs:369-378`; note core's `PrincipalId` has **only** `Object` and `Scoped` — the economy's `System` principal is `mush_org`'s, a different type, and is not reachable here), **`actor` = the domain's host object**, **`subject` = `PerceivedRef::KnownObject { id: host, revision }`**, and **`idempotency` = `IdempotencyScope::Deterministic` over `(domain, tick)`** so a duplicate driver fire is a HIT rather than a second tick. Naming this is the difference between "one commit per domain per tick" as a slogan and as something buildable.

**Resolution.** The pulse resolver does **not** create one K action per NPC, weapon or unit. It consumes the frozen set of accepted intents for the tick and produces **one deterministic domain-tick commit per domain per tick** — ordered deltas, RNG evidence, state hash. **Which cap binds, precisely** (correcting Draft 0.1's blanket claim): a tick writing one post-image per unit is bounded by **`MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_REPLAY_WRITES` = 1_024** (`authoritative.rs:27-28`) on *count*, and by **`MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_BUNDLE_BYTES` = 16 MiB** / **`MAX_DURABLE_OUTBOX_PAYLOAD_BYTES` = 256 KiB** (`:37-38`, `:35-36`) on *size*. `MAX_DURABLE_OUTBOX_INTENTS` (256) bounds what a bundle *emits*, not what a tick *accepts*, and is not the intent cap. At `MAX_UNITS_PER_DOMAIN` = 64 the write count is ~6 % of the replay-write cap, so **64 is a latency and CPU policy figure, not a K3-derived one** — the K3 caps are a hard ceiling far above it. §9 q6's measurement must report which of the three is nearest, and the pinning test fails when any of them passes 60 %.


**T2, enforced at the seam.** **No economic transaction occurs inside a combat pulse.** A tick mutates combat state (ammunition, heat, damage, position, control) over `TacticalUnit`'s existing cells, sections and installed components (`crates/mush-tactics/src/unit.rs:220-245`, `:314-326`, `:328-352`, main) and emits **typed facts at boundaries only**: sortie started/ended; unit disabled, abandoned, captured, destroyed; cargo delivered; docking completed; salvage recovered. two `+fire` in one tick are two bounded intents; an order superseded before resolution leaves only the newest active. *Restart:* a crash mid-tick converges — the tick committed whole or not at all, and re-running produces the same hash; a **duplicate tick driver fire** is a HIT on the deterministic `(domain, tick)` key and produces no second commit; a tick whose bundle would exceed any of the three caps **refuses and pages with the measured size**, it does not split silently; `state_hash` covers the accepted-intent set, the RNG root and the ordered deltas, so a replay that omits an intent fails the hash rather than reproducing it; a **`Scoped`-principal tick invocation cannot reach any command a player principal could not**, probed against the whole action surface.

**Verify lenses.** **D (determinism and replay)** — can two runs of one tick differ, or a restart change a result? **C (commit bounds)** — can a tick produce a bundle the store must refuse, and does the unit cap actually hold? **X (the T2 boundary)** — can any economic effect, RNG draw or settlement cross into a pulse?

### 3.9 Cut 9 — `mush-campaign`: declared operations, formations, resolution

**Q5D.** A **new neutral crate**; one logical operation, **separately versioned terms and runtime under one `OperationId`**. R1 is satisfied by the shared id and the one-to-one link without conflating consent with runtime. 10a's 5A is rejected because it re-learns the E4b2 lesson: every roster edit becomes either a terms amendment or a hole in the hash.

**Terms freeze; the roster does not touch them.** **Changing the roster never changes the terms hash** — a reinforcement is not an amendment to what the defenders consented to be told, and `consent_intact` (`contract.rs:1066-1068`, main) is re-checked at every transition.

**How the hash is actually shared, because Draft 0.1's "share the helper" is not buildable as written.** `consent_hash(issuer: &PrincipalId, consent: &ConsentSurface) -> [u8;32]` (`contract.rs:1014`, main) takes an **economy** `ConsentSurface` — parties, `ContractTerms`, conditions, deadline, visibility, scope, adjudicator, evidence — and its body is *sort the parties by `principal_key`, build a field-ordered canonical struct, `serde_json::to_vec`, `Sha256::digest`* (`:1015-1029`). `mush-campaign` cannot call it without either taking a `mush-economy` dependency **and** stuffing seat/operation fields into contract-shaped ones, or forking the discipline — and Q10D/10a §6 sharpening 3 forbid the fork. **The buildable reading of "share, not fork":** `mush-economy` factors the last three lines out as `pub fn consent_digest(domain: &str, canonical: &impl Serialize) -> [u8;32]`, `consent_hash` becomes a two-line wrapper over it with `domain = "contract.consent.v1"`, and `OperationTerms` and cut 10's `SeatOffer` call it with `"campaign.operation.v1"` / `"campaign.seat.v1"` over **their own** field-ordered canonical structs. One discipline, one implementation, **domain-separated** so an operation hash can never be replayed as a contract hash — which forking would not have given either. Sorting rules carry over verbatim: any principal vector sorts by `principal_key`, every other vector's order *is* consented content. This is a small, additive `mush-economy` change and belongs in §10's handbacks.

**T6's visibility classes.** `EconomicVisibility::Audience { members, announce_issuer }` with `announce_issuer` defaulting to `false` (`record.rs:339-365`, `:362-363`, main) is already T6's *"notice of the window, not the plan"*: public; eligible defenders; participating organizations; covert existence with a visible vulnerability warning; staff/audit only. **"Declared" means every party entitled to defend receives sufficient notice and knows the window — not that the attacker's plan or identity is published.** `MAX_AUDIENCE` is 32 (`record.rs:322-323`).

**The bond** is an economy hold placed by the attacker's **own** authority — never an industry reservation, because it is money (T4). S5A's ≈ 24–72 h notice is `OPERATION_NOTICE_MIN/MAX` (§7).

**Formations** (Q9A/T8/R1/R2). `composition` is a **constraint set, not one enum**, and **`exact` is `minimum == maximum`**, not a separate mode. **Strictness is scoped** — at *creation*, at *doctrine qualification* ("is this still a full squadron?"), or as *an operation's requirement* — **never to a formation's existence or its ability to deploy**. An understrength permanent formation is normal, not an error. `ruleset-tacsim` authors the instances in cut 1's packs, so a four-unit lance and a five-unit star share an echelon and remain distinct kinds. **Each node names exactly one owning `OrgScopeId`** and `force.*` capabilities resolve through the existing grant machinery (`crates/mush-org/src/authorize.rs:143-200`, main), so **R2 needs no core change**. It does need one composition-root addition: `org_capability_vocabulary()` is **non-empty** and is a typo catcher at the grant command (`crates/mush-server/src/modules.rs:176-182`, main, holds only the economy ids plus `org.role.manage` and `org.grant.delegate` — housing joins it in the in-flight E5b tree; `crates/mush-org/src/wiring.rs:78-93`), so granting an unlisted `force.*` id **fails loudly at `@org/grant`** — the ids must be added there. Administrative parentage is unchanged by attachment; **a unit is in at most one operation at a time** (R1), with exactly one operational command parent within it.

**Terminal settlement** (Q17A). An operation ends by writing **one** `campaign.operation.resolved` root fact into the mechanical journal (T2). Every consequence is a separate, **idempotent**, causally linked consumer transition keyed off that root. `Resolving { pending }` is only meaningful if the set is closed, so **`ConsumerId` is a closed engine enum, enumerated here**:

```rust
enum ConsumerId { CampaignOutcome,   // the outcome row recorded on the operation           — always
                  FrontControl,      // `mush-faction` Front/ControlRecord (lib.rs:235-274) — iff a
                                     //   Consequence names a (RegionId, ControlKind)
                  BondSettlement,    // the E4 Hold released, forfeited or split            — iff bond
                  ContractOutcomes,  // outcome selection on linked contracts (contract.rs:227-276)
                                     //                                                     — iff any

```

The **required set is computed once, at entry to `Resolving`, from the frozen terms**, and stored on the runtime — never re-derived later. That is the whole crash-safety argument and it has a second edge Draft 0.1 missed: if a later build adds a sixth consumer, **a stored `Resolving` set must not grow and a `Resolved` operation must never re-open**; an unknown stored `ConsumerId` at boot fails closed and pages level 2 rather than being dropped as unrecognised. The operation stays `Resolving` until every member acknowledges, and only then reaches `Resolved`. 10a's 17B is rejected: it claims an atomicity the tree does not have across three crates (08c §12).

**Commands.** `@op/declare <objective>` · `@op/terms <id>/<field>=<value>` (pre-activation only) · `@op/activate <id>` · `@op/join <id>=<unit|formation>` · `@op/withdraw <id>=<unit>` · `@op/show <id>` (per audience) · `@op/resolve <id>=<outcome>` · `@op/consumers <id>` (staff) · `@force/create|attach|detach|assign` · `@formation/show <id>`.

**Tests (~30).** *Freeze:* a terms edit after activation refuses; a roster edit leaves `consent_hash` byte-identical; a mutated surface makes `consent_intact` false and the operation refuses to progress — it disputes and pages rather than settling; the notice window sits inside `OPERATION_NOTICE_MIN/MAX`. *Audience:* a non-audience viewer sees no operation id, issuer or objective (the existence-hiding renderer treats it like an account id); an audience member sees the window and notice but **not** the plan when `announce_issuer` is false; the same constraint set refuses at creation and at doctrine qualification; `minimum == maximum` behaves as `exact`; a lance and a star share an echelon and stay distinct kinds. *Bond:* the bond is a `Hold` placed by the attacker's own authority; an attacker without funds cannot activate; it settles exactly once per outcome row. *Resolution:* the root fact is written once; each consumer transition is idempotent under replay; a crash after two of five consumers resumes and does not double-pay; `Resolved` is unreachable while any consumer is pending; a permanently failing consumer opens a level-2 escalation with the recommended command filled in (08c §4.6) and **suspends only the operation**. *Fronts:* a resolution advancing a front writes one `ControlRecord` change, and re-running the consumer writes none — and because `mush-faction`'s `Clock` is a **segment counter with no due instant** (`sim.rs:67-98`, main), the consumer fills segments and never restamps a deadline. *Added by the review:* one operation's permanently failing consumer **does not starve another operation's fan-out** (08c §5.2 B4's wall-of-disputes shape); adding a consumer in a later build leaves already-`Resolved` operations untouched (a probe re-boots with a sixth id registered); `@op/show` for a non-audience viewer is byte-identical for a covert operation and for an id that never existed, at every verbosity, and `@op/consumers` is staff-only; a `force.*` capability granted **before** it was added to the vocabulary still authorizes nothing (`authorize` matches by equality, `authorize.rs:143-200`) and `@org/show` flags it rather than deleting it; every operation, terms, runtime and formation record re-enters through its constructor (§4), and a stored participant list over `MAX_OPERATION_PARTICIPANTS` refuses on load.

**Verify lenses.** **Z (freeze integrity)** — can any path change what a defender consented to be told, or progress an operation whose surface no longer hashes? **A (audience)** — can a viewer learn a covert operation exists, or its plan, through any render or error? **I (idempotent fan-out)** — can a crash, retry or duplicate root fact double-apply a consequence, or strand an operation in `Resolving`?

**Hands to cut 10:** the operation scope seat grants, musters and readiness obligations attach to, and the participation runtime settlement indexes on.

### 3.10 Cut 10 — crew, proxy seats, musters, the oracle

**Crew** (C11A/C13A/C14A/C15A/T16). Named NPC objects occupy dramatically relevant positions — captain, helm, tactical, engineering, marine command; routine watchstanders, technicians, loaders, stewards and medical staff are an **aggregate complement** carrying readiness, capacity and morale. A **periodic readiness obligation** covers ordinary payroll and support — an E4b1 `Obligation` on the **operation cadence with success notifications off** (doc 10 §15) — and individual employment contracts are used only when a named NPC's personal terms matter. **T16:** the actor at a station supplies the relevant traits, and **a human proxy directing an NPC still uses the NPC's sheet** — the human adds judgment, not an absent character's stats. Any wider effect needs a causal action or changed ship state, **never a free global bonus for seat occupancy**. Crew quarters reuse E5a's tenancy shape (08d §3.5), not a new one.

**Proxy seats are consent grants, not contracts** (Q10D; doc 10 R6 **as amended**), and their `consent_hash` uses cut 9's shared `consent_digest` under the `"campaign.seat.v1"` domain — never a forked hasher, never an economy `ConsentSurface` stuffed with seat fields (§3.9). The governing fact: **an OOC proxy player is not an in-world principal.** C1A/R3 forbid an account principal and keep it out of any contract or canon record, and T13 says the absent character did not take the station — **so an E4 contract can name neither, and there is no third candidate.** 10a's 10A is rejected for exactly that reason.

```rust
struct SeatOffer { id: SeatOfferId, operation: OperationId, audience: AudienceSpec,

                   revocation: RevocationTerms, fallback: FallbackSpec, consent_hash: [u8; 32] }

                   accepted_at: ClockStamp, consent_hash: [u8; 32], state: GrantState }

```

**T11's guardrails are the granted channel set on the NPC's `StationSpec`** (`mush-sim/src/data.rs:414-435`, channels at `:426`, main), **not contract prose**. Proxy control grants tactical discretion, **not** title, administration or an unrestricted possession verb: no asset transfer or abandonment, scuttling, fallback change, loadout or crew mutation, organization spending, surrender of unrelated assets, departure from the authorized operation, or assumption of another station. **Ordinary tactical loss cannot be made impossible without making the seat fictitious.**

**Live control is a lease derived from the grant, not the grant itself.** Revocation bumps the unit's control epoch immediately (`bump_control_gen`, `mush-sim/src/data.rs:618-622`, main) so stale intents fail cut 5's epoch check, and the seat returns to its declared fallback at a safe boundary (T11).

**The K6 gate, as a predicate rather than a promise.** Draft 0.1 said the handoff is "stubbed behind the K6 gate" without saying what the stub refuses, which leaves the most dangerous case open: an NPC automation fallback holding movement and weapons channels **is** an active lease, so activating a proxy over it inside a live domain is exactly what §2.4 forbids. Until K6 lands, `Dormant → Active` is permitted **only when all three hold**, and refuses naming K6 otherwise:

1. the target station is **vacant or held by the grant's own declared `FallbackSpec`** — never by another actor;
2. the unit's `DomainClock` is **not** `Pulse` (no live domain), or the unit is not placed in a domain at all;
3. **no channel in `SeatScope.channels` is under a live control lease** — which, before K6, means no other grant on that station has been activated and not released.

When K6 lands, (2) and (3) relax to *commit the transfer at the declared safe boundary* with the exact grant and lease epoch captured (proposal §6). Until then a test pins that **E7 ships no eviction path at all**. An **E4 contract links to the operation only when an actual fictional principal** — embodied character, named NPC, organization — **has economic terms**; an unpaid proxy seat needs none, and inventing one to hold the consent hash is 10a's 10A error.

**Attribution and audit** (Q19A/T9/R7). The mechanical journal records the **in-world actor** and an **opaque decision-driver token**, and nothing else. A **server-side, audit-only** record links that token to the account; ordinary projections, renders and exports cannot reverse it, and one staff verb can. OOC participation history and matchmaking standing **consume the audit record**, live outside the world, and **never become IC settlement**: T13's absent character earns **no** pay, advancement, reputation, knowledge or property, and standing is a **crew-call eligibility policy input only** (R6).

**Focus** (Q20A/T12). OOC proxy control **consumes the account's one foreground focus** (cut 5). The player's own character remains at their durable location and **cannot act concurrently**; entering proxy mode hands off that character's station, clearly identifies the represented NPC and exposes **only that NPC station's observer projection** (cut 7). Leaving restores AI control; it never moves or protects the player's character, who is present and vulnerable under E5's attention budget and non-response semantics (C22A/R5). **Accepting an offer does not seize focus** — the grant is `Dormant` until activated.

**Musters** (T14/Q21A). E7 uses **E6a's `mush-party`** — game-neutral, `{mush-core}` deps only, not a principal, never learning about money (08f §2). **There is never a TacSim-specific party record**; if E7 lands first it pulls that exact cut forward unchanged. An operation publishes start, expected duration, committed formations, character billets, proxy-eligible NPC stations, certifications and restrictions, command succession, secrecy and compensation. At muster a slot resolves to a player character, a human-directed NPC, an AI-controlled named NPC, automation, or vacancy. **Missing players reduce discretion and resilience but do not cancel a valid operation.** Late arrival and early departure use the same explicit handoff (C29A).

**The distinctness oracle's consumers** (Q11A/R3/C23A/C24A). Roster lock and side assignment call `all_accounts_distinct` and **store only the verdict**; the private account id never enters a journal, contract or canon record. An account may materially assist **only one side** of a defined operation, battle, claim contest or similar competitive episode.

**Participation facts** (R6/T13; doc 10 §15). Settlement splits index on **participation**, which E4b2's outcome-indexed terms already support once the sim emits `sim.station.served { actor, station, from, to }`, answered from an **applied marker, never a status** (08c §6.2; 08f §7 item 3). Kill, salvage and delivery credit are the same shape.

**Commands.** `+seat/offer <operation>/<unit>/<station>` · `+seat/list` · `+seat/accept <id>` · `+seat/decline <id>` · `+seat/activate <id>` · `+seat/leave` · `@seat/revoke <id>` · `+muster <operation>` · `+crewcall/list|apply` · `@crew/complement <unit>` · `@crew/readiness <unit>` · `@seat/audit <token>` (staff only).

**Tests (~30).** *Guardrails:* each of T11's nine forbidden acts refuses from a proxy seat and succeeds from the owner; a granted channel set narrower than the station's limits the proxy exactly; a proxy cannot assume a second station; an intent outside `permitted_area` refuses; **ordinary tactical loss is permitted** — a probe asserts the seat is not made safe. *Revocation:* revocation bumps the epoch and every queued human, NPC, pathfinder and LLM intent under the old grant fails stale; the seat returns to its declared fallback **at a safe boundary**, not mid-tick; a disconnect uses the same path; a revoked grant cannot be re-activated. *K6 gate:* activating a grant over a **live** operator refuses with the gate named, and a test pins that E7 ships no eviction path. *Focus:* activating a seat releases the player's own station's control and fires its fallback; the player's character stays where it was, present and vulnerable, and cannot act; leaving restores AI control and does not move the character; accepting without activating seizes nothing. *Attribution:* the journal carries actor + driver token and **never** an account id (a recursive scan of journal bytes asserts it); the audit record resolves the token for staff and nobody else; an export cannot reverse it. *Absent-player settlement:* the absent character receives no pay, advancement, reputation, knowledge or property from a proxy sortie; canon attaches to the NPC, unit, organization and operation; matchmaking standing changes and **cannot** be spent on character power. *Distinctness:* two characters on one account fail `all_accounts_distinct` at roster lock and side assignment; an oracle answering `None` fails closed; only the verdict is stored. *Musters:* a slot resolving to vacancy does not cancel the operation; a late arrival takes a seat through the same handoff; a probe asserts no TacSim party type exists. *Readiness:* the obligation runs on the operation cadence, is silent on success, and its arrears follow E4b1's ladder. *Added by the review:* **the K6 predicate is tested as three independent refusals** — a station held by another actor, a unit in a `Pulse` domain, and a channel under a live lease each refuse alone and name K6; a `Dormant` grant on an operation that **aborts** expires rather than surviving to a later operation; a `DriverToken` is **per grant**, so the same player accepting two seats produces two tokens and neither correlates them in any projection; a grant whose **audit row is missing at boot** refuses activation and pages, it does not activate un-attributed; `+seat/list` shows nothing about an operation the viewer is not in the audience of, and a declined offer leaves no trace a third party can read; the aggregate complement's readiness arrears **stop the obligation's subject only** (08c §4.6), never the operation; every offer, grant and complement record re-enters through its constructor (§4), and `MAX_SEAT_OFFERS_PER_OPERATION` refuses on load rather than truncating.

**Verify lenses.** **G (guardrail escape)** — can a proxy reach any authority outside its channel set, area or operation? **R (revocation and boundary)** — can control persist past a revocation, be lost mid-tick, or be handed off before the boundary? **A (audit irreversibility)** — can the account behind a driver token be recovered by anything but the staff verb, or reach a journal, contract or canon record?

**Hands to cut 11:** a crewed, operationally committed force the proving loop can run, and the participation facts settlement indexes on.

### 3.11 Cut 11 — the recovery provider, the corridor seed pack, the rate policy

**The recovery provider** (Q7A/T7; doc 10 S3A). **No new engine machinery.** The provider is a **government or corporate organization principal** owning the loaner inventory, accounts, contracts and policy. A system faucet may **subsidize** it under the `[economy]` mint cap via `create_system_obligation` (`crates/mush-economy/src/api.rs:1105-1203`, main) — **a `System` principal can never itself be a contract party** (08f D-E6-4, which the tree forces). **Eligibility is a deterministic predicate over recorded facts examining accessible operational assets** — unencumbered, reachable, deployable — **rather than total title**, so a player whose only hull is an unreachable wreck still qualifies. **Staff are never the gate.** The grant is a contract placing `Encumbrance::Lease` on the loaner (`record.rs:528-536`, main), with upkeep an `Obligation` on the campaign cadence and restrictions as ordinary conditions. Q7A requires the plan to state five things, and it does:

| Question (Q7A) | Answer |
|---|---|
| Pool exhaustion | the queue holds; the provider stops granting and pages **level 1** — an empty pool is not a decision |
| Queue priority | first-eligible, then longest-waiting; ties by claim time |
| Per-player cooldown | `RECOVERY_COOLDOWN` after a lease ends, waived when the loss was inside the restrictions |
| Insurance | approved operations only; outside them the lease is uninsured and a loss becomes a debt |
| Replenishment | the provider's own production work orders (cut 4) and salvage recovery, funded by its budget — **never a mint into the pool** |
| Upgrade / loss | buy out at a declared price or re-lease one tier up; loss inside the restrictions is written off, outside it becomes a debt |

**The corridor seed pack** (Q6A/Q18A/doc 10 §11). The full Mars–Phobos–Ceres content as **data**: propulsion and route tables, the ten commodity families, component definitions, recipes, formation kinds, combat formulas, insurance and warfare policy in **rules-definition packs**; the corridor locations, the player corporation, the government faction and its rival, the refinery, the mining drones, the hauler, the patrol craft, the dropship, the infantry, the tank and the mech in a **campaign-seed pack**. **No hot mutation** (Q18A): a change publishes a new version that coexists, and live units, blueprints, recipes and open contracts stay **pinned** until an explicit migration or in-world refit — **also T1's guarantee that a published hauling contract's route assumptions cannot shift underneath it.** **The sale path is an ordinary E4b2 contract** between the seller and a funded NPC corporation principal — consideration in `InstrumentKind::{Currency, Commodity}`, a `Before` deadline, the physical hand-over an attributed cut-2 inventory act linked back by `ExternalRef` and gated by an `inventory.transferred` fact (D-E7-31). No listing, no quote, no vendor record, no order book: all four are E6c's (§1.1).

**The timeline's rate policy** (Q8D). **The kernel owns generations** (K4a, §2.1); **the campaign layer sets rate policy** — doc 10 S1A's ≈ one OOC hour per IC day is a **policy value, not a constant in code**. ## 4. Build discipline

As 08c §7 and 08a's standing rhythm, per cut: (1) **confirm the cut's slice of this plan against the tree it is built on** — after the merge that is one tree, and every *(K)*-marked line number is re-checked once at cut 1; (2) build, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`, `cargo test --workspace`; (3) **a probe-confirming adversarial verify of that cut** — the lenses named in its section, throwaway probe tests — **before the next cut builds on it**; (4) fold every confirmed finding with a regression test named `vf_<lens><n>_…`; (5) commit with the verify outcome in the message; **push only on the owner's word**. Both digest pins (E2 `9c917eee…`, E3-shaped `b780e3a0…`) stay green in every cut, and **`cargo fmt` stays off** (08a).

**The production-slice proposal's §9 review checklist is adopted as E7's standing verify checklist**, quoted in full and applied to every cut:

> - No command or timer can confirm before its durable acknowledgement.
> - No `?` or other fallible operation remains after the first irreversible live mutation.
> - Every retryable entry has a bounded scoped idempotency key and semantic request hash.
> - Holds subtract from availability and facility capacity; embedded advisory counters are not authority.
> - Rate changes create generations; old facts retain old generations.
> - Channels and priorities are never unioned across station grants.
> - Title/custody/location/station/grant/lease/maintenance remain separate facts.
> - Completion cannot expose an object before all required owner projections agree.
> - All lists, catch-up loops, recipes, inputs, outputs, reservations, events, and DTO bytes are bounded.
> - Staff intervention is the last resort after deterministic reconciliation and scoped recovery have been exhausted; any page includes the exact order, failed invariant, and recommended runbook.

The last item is 08c §4.6's ladder in the proposal's words, and both are binding: **resolve programmatically wherever the best action is determinable; page a human only for a genuine decision; stop only the subject; never stop the world for a local problem.** The sixth item is why §2.2's main-vs-K difference makes the merge a precondition rather than a preference.

**Five standing rules the review added, because Draft 0.1 stated each in one cut and needed them in all eleven.**

1. **Every persisted record uses the `TryFrom` wire.** `#[serde(try_from = "…Wire")]` through the type's own constructor, so a stored document re-checks every cap and invariant on load. A derived `Deserialize` walks straight past them and makes §7 advisory — the exact E5a finding (08d §3.6 A2/A3); the in-tree precedents are `mush-core`'s `DefinitionRef`, `TacticalUnit` (`unit.rs:362`) and `ActionInvocation` (`action.rs:686`).
2. **Every cut states its boot rule.** For each record: which documents boot reads, what a malformed one does (**skipped loudly and its subject marked**, never silently zeroed or dropped), what a dangling reference does, and what is *quarantined* rather than refused. Boot never trusts a stored surface (08c §5.2 B6).
3. **Every retryable entry names its key shape in this document, not in the code review.** Cut 1 `tacsim:seed:{pack}@{version}`; cut 2 the reservation holder key; cut 3 `extract/{deposit}/{cycle}`; cut 4 the work order's `wo/{id}/{verb}` at a revision generation; cut 6 `journey/{carrier}/{leg}`; cut 8 `domain/{id}/tick/{n}`; cut 9 `op/{id}/{consumer}`; cut 10 `seat/{offer}/{verb}`. All are `IdempotencyKey::Deterministic` and all must render inside `MAX_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_BYTES` (128).
4. **One internal view feeds every projection.** The line render and any DTO come from the same function; DTO bytes are bounded; a refusal renders through the hiding renderer, never raw (08c §5.2 A2, §6.2 D1–D8). Two refusals a viewer may not distinguish must be **byte-identical**, not merely similar.
5. **LLMs explain and select; they never invent** (proposal §3, doc 10 §7). No LLM path may originate a recipe, reservation, price, capability, destination, grant, contact or completion result; an LLM-sourced intent is an ordinary intent under an ordinary grant with its own driver attribution, and cut 10's revocation test covers it explicitly.

## 9. Open questions, renumbered (defaults chosen)

Only what 10a and doc 10 leave open. Each default is what the build takes unless the owner says otherwise. **Three of Draft 0.1's thirteen are now closed** and are recorded under §11 instead: old q5 (T4 already decides it), old q11 (resolved by naming an owner) and old q13 (resolved to *merge, then develop on main*). **Items marked ★ are owner decisions in disguise** — they move scope, money or staff burden across a programme boundary, and a default is not the right instrument for them.

1. **The active-domain limit.** Q15A requires a hard limit and a stated policy but sets neither. Default **`MAX_ACTIVE_DOMAINS` 8**, and at the limit the trigger **queues** — held and re-evaluated each campaign tick, never silently dropped — because "refuse with notice" strands an interception and "degrade to scheduled resolution" is a second combat model. Config, so a host can raise it.
2. **Tick rates per domain class.** S2A gives bands, not values. Default **ground 2 s, space 6 s**, both config, both inside the bands.
3. **The pack digest algorithm.** Default **SHA-256**, matching `plan_hash` (08c §3.3) and the existing `DefinitionRef` digest discipline, with the domain/encoding version stated in the manifest rather than in the hashed bytes (doc 09 §2.6), and the manifest's own `digest` field excluded from the hashed bytes.
4. **The interim for object-per-unit materials before cut 2.** Default: **there is none.** Cut 1's fixture ships locations, commodity *definitions*, a recipe and a route but **no quantities**, and nothing consumes matter until `mush-inventory` exists. An object-per-unit interim would be a second quantity authority — exactly what Q13A exists to prevent — and would need migrating out one cut later.
5. **Where the account-identity seam ships.** 10a §4 lists "the Q11 oracle" under both cut 5 and cut 10. Default: **cut 5 ships the seam plus the focus claim/release half** (it cannot enforce one focus without it); **cut 10 adds `same_account`/`all_accounts_distinct`** and their consumers.
6. **How `MAX_UNITS_PER_DOMAIN` is derived.** Default: **measure at cut 8** — encode a worst-case tick for N units, report which of `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_REPLAY_WRITES`, `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_BUNDLE_BYTES` and `MAX_DURABLE_OUTBOX_PAYLOAD_BYTES` is nearest, and pin the constant where the encoded bundle stays under 60 % of it, with a test that fails when a change pushes past. 64 is the working figure and is a **latency** choice, not a K3-derived one (§3.8).
7. ★ **If E6a has not shipped `mush-party` when cut 10 starts.** Default: **pull E6a's exact cut forward unchanged** (Q21A's own instruction), never a temporary record. **Flagged:** this moves a cut *out of* E6a and into E7's budget and schedule, and changes which programme owns `mush-party` at review time. That is a programme-scope call, not a build default.
8. **Where the campaign's restamp-policy registry lives.** Default: **`mush-campaign`** (cut 9), with cut 4 shipping the pause default and the registration seam, so cuts 4–8 run correctly before the campaign layer exists.
9. **Whether `mush-industry` and `mush-inventory` are one crate.** Default: **two.** Q13A's neutrality argument is that market, contract deliverables and salvage need quantity truth without an industry dependency; one crate would give them one.
10. **The ten commodity families' exact ids.** Default: S4A's list transliterated — `metals`, `volatiles`, `carbon_feedstock`, `strategic_elements`, `structural_alloys`, `composites`, `electronics`, `propellant`, `industrial_parts`, `munitions` — authored in cut 1's rules pack and therefore changeable without code.
11. ★ **The scene IC time floor's owner** (was q11, now sharpened rather than defaulted). The *shape* is fixed here so the two candidates cannot diverge: a monotonic `ic_floor` on `SceneRecord` that `scene_ic_time` clamps to (`mush-scenes/src/data.rs:175-178`, main), raised by a journey arrival alongside a typed arrival event, `SceneTurn` as the carrier. The *owner* is a real choice: **E6b wants it** for §4.1's `ic_time` workaround (08f §7 item 4) and **cut 6 cannot be correct without it** (S6A). Default: **E6b ships it if E6b lands first; otherwise cut 6 does** — and either way cut 6 owns the arrival raise. **Flagged** because Draft 0.1 justified "whichever lands first" by citing 08f §11 q2, which had done the opposite: it *resolved* the shared `@econ/cadence` question by **naming E5b**. The precedent argues for naming an owner, not for leaving it to arrival order.
12. ★ **Whether the corridor waits for E6c's vendor path.** Default: **no** — cuts 3 and 11 sell through a plain E4b2 contract with a funded NPC corporation (D-E7-31), which is landed today and needs nothing from E6. **Flagged:** the alternative is to slip cuts 3 and 11 behind E6c so the corridor's first sale is a real listing, which is a schedule and content decision, not a technical one. Either way §11's "one open market" is incomplete until E6c.
13. ★ **Whether cut 10 is worth building before K6.** With the K6 predicate stated (§3.10), cut 10 ships offers, grants, fallback, attribution, musters and the distinctness oracle, but a proxy can take a seat **only** on a unit that is not in a live domain and whose channels are unleased — which is not the case T10 was written for. Default: **build it anyway**, because the consent record, the guardrails and the audit seam are the expensive half and K6 then adds only the boundary transfer. **Flagged** because the owner may prefer cut 10 to wait for K6 and cut 11 to run with NPC and automation crewing only.

### 11.1 What was wrong and is now changed

**HIGH.**

1. **The corridor had no sale path.** §1.1, §3 and D-E7-26 said the loop runs on "direct vendor sale (E6b's vendor-from-stock path)". No such E6b path exists: `Supply::Stock { vault }`, quotes and vendors are all **E6c's** (08f §2's cut table, §5.2 *"Listings, quotes, vendors"*), so the physical loop had nothing to sell into from cut 3 to cut 11. 10a §4 dep 1 made the error first and Draft 0.1 inherited it. Cut 11 no longer depends on E6c.
2. **"No E7 cut blocks on a K item" was false, and its supporting citation was wrong.** Cut 5 needs K5a's one-grant-per-station projection *and* an epoch comparison. `mush-sim/src/data.rs:635-640` — cited by both Draft 0.1 and 10a as "the sim-side staleness check is already the control epoch" — is the **read accessor** `control_generation`; **no epoch comparison exists anywhere in `crates/*/src`** (G4).
