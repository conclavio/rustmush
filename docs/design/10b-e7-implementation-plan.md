## 1. Scope — what E7 is, in the owner's terms

Doc 10 §1 is a **solar-system industrial warfare sandbox** where every battle has an economic cause. Doc 10 §11 names the first playable corridor — **Mars–Phobos–Ceres** — and its proving story: *extract water and metals near Ceres → hire players to haul to Phobos → fight or evade an interception → manufacture or refit a ground unit → load it aboard a carrier → deploy to Mars → complete a ground operation that changes control or access → salvage, repair, pay, repeat.* **Q22A** bounds E7 to **one coherent narrow vertical slice**: that loop end to end, with **one ground engagement and one local-space interception**, both fought with **authored** units from the rules pack. E7 builds:

- **A world made of packs, not code** (Q6A/Q18A) — immutable digest-referenced rules packs and once-applied campaign-seed packs; only what a table cannot express is Rust.
- **Quantity truth with a location** (Q2D/Q13A) — a neutral `mush-inventory` owning location-bound pools *and* lineage-bearing lots, every balance on an **E3 custody endpoint**, never a locationless account balance.
- **Industry that plans legibly** (Q1A/Q14A/Q3A) — deposits with deterministic curves and revealed *bands*; `ClaimRecord` tenure with licences as entitlements; facilities, recipes and work orders reserving matter, money and capacity through a **C8 shape-conformant** record.
- **One character, one active station** (C2A/C4A/C5A/Q20A) — billets stay many; the live control surface is the one focused station's K5a `ChannelGrant`, never a union.
- **Voyages as inhabited play** (C16A/C20A/Q15A) — the carrier root travels with its interiors, occupants, cargo and carried craft coherent; an interception **promotes** it into a live domain and demotes it at a quiet boundary with a durable summary.
- **One propagation layer** (Q4A/Q16A/R4) — detection and delivery over one physical context; contacts belong to a platform, never a side; orders are envelopes that must actually arrive.
- **A deterministic pulse** (S2A/Q12A/T2) — `DomainClock::Pulse`, tactical ingress on the prepared K lane, **one commit per domain per tick**, and no economic transaction inside a pulse.
- **Declared operations that settle** (Q5D/T6/Q9A/Q17A) — frozen terms plus mutable runtime under one `OperationId`, formations as constraint sets bound to org scopes, a bond as an economy hold, one journaled resolution root fact with idempotent consumers.
- **Crew and seats** (C11A/C15A/T10–T16/Q10D) — named decision NPCs plus an aggregate complement, readiness on the operation cadence, **consent-grant** proxy seats with dual attribution and deterministic fallback.
- **A floor under loss** (S3A/Q7A/T7) — a funded recovery organization leasing loaners against an eligibility test over *accessible* assets, never a faucet.

**Exit criterion.** Doc 10 §11's proving loop runs end to end on the seed pack, with the ground engagement and the local-space interception both resolving through the pulse driver and settling through `campaign.operation.resolved`.

### 2.3 K3 audit closures E7 may assume *(K)*

- **K3.1a — checkpoint proof (closed):** memory and Redis atomically verify every exact replay post-image **before** deleting the full intent; a mismatch retains the intent and fails closed (`09:2057-2058`).
- **K3.1b — store-owned admission (closed):** the store refuses a second unresolved authoritative reservation, so two pending bundles cannot claim the same fresh object id (`09:2059-2061`).
- Bundle hard caps — `crates/mush-core/src/authoritative.rs:27-38` *(main)*: replay writes 1_024 (`:27-28`), definitions 256 (`:29-30`), witnesses 1_024 (`:31-32`), durable-outbox intents 256 (`:33-34`), payload 256 KiB (`:35-36`), bundle 16 MiB (`:37-38`). Q12A and 10a §6 sharpening 4 call these "the real cap on a domain's active unit count"; **that is true of the tick commit, but only three of the six can bind it** — `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_REPLAY_WRITES` (1_024) bounds *how many* per-unit post-images a tick may write, `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_BUNDLE_BYTES` (16 MiB) and `MAX_DURABLE_OUTBOX_PAYLOAD_BYTES` (256 KiB) bound *how large* they may be. The other three are irrelevant to a tick. ### 2.4 The three kernel-migration gates, and the K6 control gate

Before **any** production reservation or settlement moves off the economy journal onto authoritative K3 state families, all three must land (proposal §2.3; 08c §12's K3 review, `08c:1793-1813` *(main)*, the three gates named at `:1804-1805`): (1) an authoritative bundle can carry the **mixed owner write set**, including the Ledger append; (2) ordinary module code can **synchronously read its typed state families outside an action**; (3) the **Ledger seal is folded into the same exact bundle** rather than acting as an external predecessor barrier. **Status on `91208ab`** (08i §2 rows 13 and 26; answers 13 and 15, G-B): **gate 2 is closed** — `Ctx::action_state` / `Ctx::scan_action_state` (`crates/mush-core/src/module.rs`), owner-scoped, work in commands, module ticks, boot callbacks, observers and contextual hooks — **except** inside `move_attempt`, `carry_attempt`, rendering and perception hooks, for which K adds a narrow immutable hook-read context (G-B); no cut 1–11 reads typed state from those hooks. **Gates 1 and 3 are open and now owned**: K names them **K8.0** — idempotently replayable Ledger materialization, the seal inside the authoritative bundle, memory/Redis conformance, failure/restart probes — sequenced before K8.1 (answer 13); 08i's E fallback draft for them is withdrawn. `crates/mush-core/src/authoritative.rs` `ReplayWrite` has no Ledger variant and `flush_prepared_action` still rejects on a non-empty `RetainedSeals`.

### 3.1 Cut 1 — `ruleset-tacsim` skeleton and the corridor fixture

**Records** (Q6A: everything expressible as a table is a versioned TOML content pack, in two kinds).

**Two corrections to the block above, from cut 1's §4.0 pass and the owner's OPEN rulings (2026-09-05).** (a) **`RouteDef` and `LegDef` are `mush-sim`'s types, not `ruleset-tacsim`'s** (OPEN 2). `mush-sim → {core, economy, proto}` and every ruleset depends on `mush-sim`, so `mush-sim → ruleset-tacsim` would be a cycle and the cut as drafted is unbuildable. The **type** goes in `crates/mush-sim/src/route.rs` beside `Leg`, behind a `TryFrom` wire, adding **no crate edge**; the **data** is authored in the tacsim rules pack and loaded by `ruleset-tacsim` — `crates/mush-tactics/src/mobility.rs`' stated pattern, *the crate owns the state machine, the ruleset provides the data*. `endpoints` is `(ObjId, ObjId)`, since `LocationId` does not exist and a place in this tree is an object; the pack authors them symbolically and the seed resolves them. (b) **the seed document key carries the version** — `tacsim:seed:{pack}@{version}`, §4 rule 3's shape, because invariant (ii) is "at most once per `(pack, version)`" (OPEN 5). The comment above saying `tacsim:seed:{pack}` is struck.

A **rules-definition pack** is immutable and referenced by digest: propulsion and route tables, the ten commodity families (S4A), component definitions, recipes, formation kinds (Q9A), combat and insurance policy. A **campaign-seed pack** instantiates mutable world state **once**: corridor locations, organizations, deposits, facilities, inventories, NPCs. The distinction is load-bearing — *a rules pack is re-pinned and diffed, while re-running a seed pack would duplicate a mine* (Q6A). `[tacsim.*]` host config carries deployment switches and pack paths only, mirroring `[shadowrun.economy].catalog_paths` (08f §9).

**`RouteDef` closes G1.** There is no route type in the tree: a journey's route is only `<room>[:secs]` tokens parsed in `route_create` (`crates/mush-sim/src/commands.rs` — the symbol; the `:1584-1648` cite is off by one at the start and is historical) over `Leg { to, travel_s }` (`route.rs`). T1 needs a *definition* — route cost and duration must be deterministic functions of the route definition, drive and payload, declared departure, propellant plan, and the body-pair alignment band on a campaign cadence — so the **type** lands in `mush-sim` (correction (a) above), the **data** is pack data from cut 1, and cut 6 is what makes journeys consume it. Cut 1 changes no existing type: `Leg`, `Journey`, `RouteState` and `route_create` are untouched.

**Invariants.** (i) A published digest is never mutated; a change publishes a **new version that coexists** (Q18A), and live units, blueprints, recipes and open contracts stay **pinned** until an explicit migration or in-world refit — T1's guarantee that a published hauling contract's route assumptions cannot shift underneath it. (ii) A seed pack applies at most once per `(pack, version)`; the `SeedApplication` document is the idempotency latch, written in the same batch as the objects. (iii) Loading is deterministic — same bytes, same digest, same iteration order. (iv) An unknown, oversized or digest-mismatched pack **fails closed at boot** and names the file. The engine already enforces the shape: `DomainRecord.driver` is an exact digest-bearing `DefinitionRef` (`crates/mush-sim/src/data.rs:227-232`, main) and K3 validates exact descriptors at registration (`crates/mush-core/src/module.rs:622-634`, main).

Enough for cuts 2–4 to run and for the owner to see output first.

**Commands.** `@tacsim/pack list|show <id>|verify` · `@tacsim/seed <pack> [dry|commit]` (staff) · `@tacsim/pin <subject>=<pack>@<version>` (staff, for a migration or refit).

**`ruleset-tacsim` is not a `[rulesets]` id** (owner, 2026-09-05, overriding OPEN 3's default and closing contradiction (vi)). `ruleset_modules` knows `"wod"` and `"shadowrun"` only and **refuses more than one** — that guard is unchanged, and `[rulesets].enabled` stays exactly one character ruleset. Doc 10 §20 is why: *TacSim supplies physical and civil context, not ruleset metaphysics … a ruleset decides what those facts mean to its characters.* So `TacsimRuleset` is a **`GameModule` with no `SheetSchema`** (`mush_ruleset::Ruleset` requires one, and cut 1 has none), enabled by **`[tacsim].enabled`** independently of `[rulesets].enabled`, and composed **after** the character ruleset so it can read that ruleset's facts and the ruleset can read TacSim's environmental facts through the generic seam. Its order position is pinned by a boot-order test. The crate keeps doc 10's name because its packs carry rules. `npc_agent_adapter` still answers `None` for it, for a new reason: it is not a ruleset id at all.

**Tests (~22).** *Digest:* identical bytes → identical digest; a whitespace-only edit changes it; a reordered table does not (canonical encoding); `MAX_PACK_ENTRIES` refuses. *Load:* two loads are byte-identical in memory; an unknown key, a missing `requires` dependency and a `requires` version mismatch each fail closed naming the file. *Seed:* `dry` writes nothing and lists every planned object; `commit` applies once; a second `commit` is a HIT writing nothing; a *different version* of the same pack is refused with the applied version named; a crash between object writes and the latch converges on reboot. *Pinning:* a subject pinned to `v1` is unaffected by publishing `v2`; both versions load concurrently; an unpinned consumer of a retired version fails closed rather than silently upgrading. *Routes:* a `RouteDef`'s duration is a pure function of definition, drive and payload; two evaluations agree; an unknown drive class refuses. *Fixture:* the three locations, two commodities, recipe and route exist after `commit` and nowhere else; the fixture is reproducible from an empty store; boot with a corrupt pack refuses to start. *Added by the review, and rewritten by cut 1's §4.0 pass (OPEN 5, closing contradiction (viii)):* ~~two `commit`s racing (two processes, one store)~~ — **the world is a single writer, so "two processes, one store" is not a reachable race.** The two reachable ones are asserted instead: **two `@tacsim/seed … commit` invocations in one world**, serialized by that single writer, leaving exactly one applied set and one latch with the loser a **HIT** rather than a partial write; and **a reboot after commit**, which converges because the objects and the latch ride **one `WriteBatch`** (`World::create` marks dirty, `Ctx::persist` queues a `ModuleOp`, and the world task's flush folds `take_dirty()` and `take_module_ops()` into a single batch), so a restart sees **both or neither**. A partial write is unreachable by construction and a test pins that it stays so. Also: a pack whose bytes parse but whose declared digest was computed over **non-canonical** bytes is refused at load, not at first use; the digest covers the canonical encoding and **not** the manifest's own `digest` field (a self-referential hash is unverifiable); every `PackManifest`, `RulesPack` and `SeedApplication` **re-enters through its constructor on load** (`TryFrom` wire, §4) and a stored pack over `MAX_PACK_ENTRIES` is refused rather than truncated; `@tacsim/pack show` on an unknown id and on a **known but unreadable** id answer identically (no pack-existence oracle for a non-staff viewer); `@tacsim/seed` on a store already carrying a *different* pack's objects applies only its own and names nothing about theirs.

**Verify lenses.** **P (pack identity)** — can two byte streams produce one digest, or one stream two? **S (seed idempotency)** — can any interleaving or crash duplicate a seeded object or lose the latch? **B (boot fail-closed)** — does any malformed pack degrade silently instead of refusing?

**Hands to cut 2:** the commodity family list, `alloy_smelt@1`, the `RouteDef` type, and the digest discipline every later definition reference uses.

**The digest, and exactly what it covers.** `pack_digest` is a thin wrapper over `DefinitionDigest::for_canonical_value_with_limit(PACK_DIGEST_DOMAIN, value, MAX_PACK_BYTES)` — the function `mush-core` already had. **Cut 1 defines no second digest and no second canonical encoder.** The hashed value is built by one function, `RulesPack::canonical_value`, and is exactly `{ "manifest": { id, version, kind, requires }, "entries": { id → entry } }`. *Hashed:* the pack id, the version, the kind, the ordered `requires`, and every entry. *Not hashed:* the manifest's own `digest` field (a self-referential hash is unverifiable), and the encoding version — which rides the **domain tag**, length-prefixed by `hash_parts`, per doc 09 §2.6.

*How one stream cannot make two digests:* `pack_digest` is pure over a `serde_json::Value`, the entries are a `BTreeMap` so iteration is id-ordered, and `canonical_json_bytes_with_limit` is the shared canonical encoder — no map ordering, no float, no locale and no clock enters it. Two loads of one file are asserted byte-identical in memory *and* byte-identical re-encoded. *How two streams cannot make one:* the comparison at load is against the **content's** digest, so a declaration computed over any other bytes — including the file's own raw bytes, which is the mistake an author actually makes — is refused **at load** naming both digests, and the domain tag makes a pack digest differ from a same-bytes digest under any other domain. The one thing that deliberately *does* collide is **formatting**: reordering the entries, reordering the keys inside an entry, and reflowing whitespace all produce one digest, because what is hashed is content. A whitespace edit **inside a value** changes it. That is the only self-consistent reading of §3.1's digest list — hashing raw file bytes would make a reordered table change the digest, which the same list forbids — and both halves are asserted.

**The store's document table** (the module's own doc-comment carries it in full):

| doc | class | corrupt → | direction of the loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tacsim:pack:{id}@{version}` | ledger | **skipped loudly**, world boots | the **key** still counts, so the version stays *published* and cannot be silently re-published under other bytes; boot repairs the row from the loaded pack |
| `tacsim:seed:{pack}@{version}` | latch | **skipped loudly**, world boots | the **key** still bars a re-seed. This is the one place where losing the body must not lose the fact: reading "cannot parse" as "not applied" would duplicate a mine |
| `tacsim:pin:{subject}` | pin | **skipped loudly**, world boots | a lost pin does **not** float its subject onto a newer version — resolution refuses instead (invariant (i)) |
| `tacsim:meta` | allocators | tolerated field by field | floors are re-derived from every parsed key, so losing it costs nothing (E6b's R8) |

Every one of the three is behind a `TryFrom` wire re-running its constructor with documented caps, and the **key-derived** floors (`published_keys`, `seeded_keys`) are E6b's R8 finding applied where it bites hardest: the latch is the key, not the body.

**The seed latch's one-batch argument.** `apply` creates its objects through `World::create` (which marks them dirty) and writes the latch through `Ctx::persist` (which queues a `ModuleOp`); `mush-server`'s `flush_dirty` folds `take_dirty()` and `take_module_ops()` into **one** `WriteBatch` before it reaches the store. A reboot therefore sees the objects **and** the latch or neither, and a test asserts exactly that round trip. The only remaining race is two `@tacsim/seed … commit` invocations, and the world is a single writer: they serialize, and the loser is a **HIT** that writes nothing (asserted from two different staff actors, with `take_module_ops()` empty afterwards). *What could break it:* `apply` performs the only fallible work — the owner pre-flight and the digest lookup — **before** the first `World::create`, so no `?` survives into the irreversible half; the one structurally-unreachable create failure is handled rather than propagated, latching **exactly what exists** and paging level 2 with the command filled in, because a partial seed recorded honestly and never re-run is the fail-closed direction and a re-run would duplicate a mine.

### 3.2 Cut 2 — `mush-inventory`, quantity truth with a location

**Ov, stated.** The proposal §4 gives `WorkOrder` a `material_source: Option<AssetContainerRef>` and speaks of a "material vault". **Q13A/Q2D override this**: quantities are neither asset containers nor account balances. A neutral `mush-inventory` is *the single source of quantity truth*, bound to **custody endpoints**, and industry, markets, contracts and simulation consume it — **none keeps a private stock number**. The proposal's material-vault quantities live here; `material_source` becomes an `EndpointRef`.

**Records** (Q2D fixes the fields exactly: **not** `legal`, **not** `custody`).

```rust
struct EndpointRef { provider: OwnerId, object: ObjId }     // an E3 custody endpoint
struct Pool  { endpoint: EndpointRef, commodity: CanonicalId, quantity: u64, revision: u64 }
struct Lot   { id: LotId, commodity: CanonicalId, quantity: u64, grade: Grade, lineage: Lineage }
enum  Lineage { Origin { source: ExternalRef }, Split { parent: LotId },
                Merge { parents: Vec<LotId> },              // ≤ MAX_LOT_PARENTS
                Tranche { tranche: TrancheId } }
struct PoolTranche { id: TrancheId, endpoint: EndpointRef, commodity: CanonicalId,
                     absorbed: Vec<LotId>, quantity: u64 }  // immutable lineage root
struct InventoryReservation {                               // doc 09 C8, exact fields (09:997-1006)
    id: ReservationId, owner: OwnerId, resource: ResourceRef, holder: ScopedIdempotencyKey,
    amount: u64, revision: u64, expires: ClockStamp,
    state: ReservationState,                                // Held | Consumed(commit) | Released | Expired
}
// ReservationId MUST render to a canonical `IdempotencyKeyText` (≤ MAX_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_BYTES
// = 128, `action.rs:31`, `:296-320`), because that is the only shape
// `AuthorityGuard::Reservation { provider, id, revision }` can name (`action.rs:1060-1064`).
```

**Custody is the endpoint's, not the lot's** (Q2D): a lot has one location and one custodian because its endpoint does. **Stolen or disputed status follows the *title* record** (`Encumbrance::Disputed`, `record.rs:521-527`, main; E6b's `report_stolen`). **Embargo legality is a jurisdiction/policy test at transaction time**, not a field travelling with matter — the same cargo is legal one hop away. **Licensing is the acting principal's entitlement**, not the cargo's (Q1A/Q14A).

**Verbs.** `credit`/`debit` a pool · `split(lot, n)` · `merge(lots)` · `transfer(what, to_endpoint)` · `reserve`/`consume(commit)`/`release`/`expire`. Interconversion is **one journaled, attributed act each way** (Q2D/T5): `dissolve(lot)` creates or updates an immutable `PoolTranche` and credits the pool; `crystallize(pool, n)` mints a lot whose **parent is that tranche**. **Lineage is never discarded — only coarsened in the active projection.**

**Invariants.** (1) Conservation under checked arithmetic; a `debit` below available (balance minus live holds) refuses. (2) Locality: no verb moves quantity except *between endpoints*; there is no endpoint-free balance. (3) The custody rules are E3's, unchanged — exactly one claiming provider (`custody.rs:103-131`), validate-then-mutate (`:195-226`); a hold that moves with a ship needs no second mechanism. (4) Lineage is append-only and acyclic, bounded by `MAX_LOT_PARENTS`/`MAX_LINEAGE_DEPTH`. (5) The reservation state machine is doc 09 C8's exactly; availability subtracts every live hold; the consuming action verifies holder **and** revision and applies the post-image in the same batch. (6) `mush-inventory` registers an `AuthorityGuardKind::Reservation` validator under its **own** `OwnerId`, through `ModuleRegistry::authority_guard_validator` (`mush-core/src/module.rs:1792-1800`, main); the registry is keyed `(OwnerId, AuthorityGuardKind)` (`action.rs:1420`, `:1520`) and routed per key (`:1534-1561`), so cut 4 registering the *same kind* under `mush-industry`'s `OwnerId` is legal by construction, not by luck. **There is no `brace` pattern to copy** (G3): `brace` registers definition validators, never an authority-guard validator, and no production module registers one. (7) Every record above uses the `TryFrom` wire (§4).

**Commands.** `@inv/show <endpoint>` · `@inv/lots <endpoint> [commodity]` · `@inv/lot <id>` (lineage, paged) · `@inv/move <what>=<endpoint>` · `@inv/split <lot>=<n>` · `@inv/merge <lot>,<lot>…` · `@inv/dissolve <lot>` · `@inv/crystallize <endpoint>/<commodity>=<n>` · `@inv/reservations <endpoint>`.

**Tests (~28).** *Conservation:* every verb sums; a `debit` past available refuses and writes nothing; `u64` overflow on merge refuses; a zero-quantity split refuses. *Lineage:* split → merge → split reconstructs both parents; `dissolve` then `crystallize` leaves the tranche as the child's parent and the original lot id reachable; a merge over `MAX_LOT_PARENTS` refuses; a cycle is unconstructible; coarsening the projection deletes no stored parent; `MAX_LINEAGE_DEPTH` refuses. *Locality:* no verb produces an endpoint-free pool; moving a ship moves its hold's contents by construction; a transfer to a destroyed endpoint refuses; a transfer between endpoints claimed by two providers refuses. *Reservations:* two orders cannot hold one unit; `consume` twice with the same commit is a HIT; a stale revision refuses; `release` after `consume` refuses; expiry is idempotent and survives restart; availability is balance minus live holds at every read. *Restart:* every record re-enters through its constructor on load (E5a lens-A: deserialization must not walk past the caps); a corrupt lot document is skipped **loudly** and its endpoint marked, never silently zeroed. *Custody:* a lot in a vault inherits the vault's access; `may_take` refuses a non-custodian. *Added by the review:* a **corrupt lot skipped loudly must not raise availability** — the endpoint is marked and every `debit`/`reserve` against it refuses until reconciled, because the fail-open shape here is "the missing lot was the one the hold was against" (E5a lens A); a `Consumed` reservation replayed with the **same commit token under a different holder** refuses rather than HITs; availability read *between* the two writes of a partially applied batch never exceeds the pre-image; a `ReservationId` that will not render as `IdempotencyKeyText` is refused at construction, not at guard time; `@inv/show` on an endpoint the viewer may not read is byte-identical to `@inv/show` on one that does not exist; a `transfer` whose source and destination are the same endpoint is a no-op HIT, not a double credit; a reservation whose `expires` stamp is on a **different clock generation** refuses comparison rather than expiring early (K4a `checked_cmp`).

**Verify lenses.** **C (conservation and lineage)** — can any sequence create, destroy or orphan quantity, or lose a parent? **L (locality)** — can a balance exist without a live endpoint, or move without a custody act? **R (reservation)** — can two holders spend one unit, or a `Consumed` hold be released?

**Hands to cut 3:** the endpoint-bound pool/lot API extraction writes into, and the C8-conformant reservation shape cut 4 reuses for capacity.

### 3.3 Cut 3 — `mush-industry`: deposits, claims, survey, extraction

**Records** (Q1A and Q14A, verbatim field lists). `Deposit` is `mush-industry`'s; **`mush-economy` already owns an unrelated `DepositRecord`** (`contract.rs:567`, escrow bookkeeping) and neither name may drift into the other crate.

A **licence is not tenure**: it is an `InstrumentKind::Entitlement` account issued by a government principal (`record.rs:50-61`, main) with campaign-cadence expiry, proving **authorization** to survey, extract or export — checked **at transaction time** against the *acting principal* (Q1A/Q14A/Q2D).

**Yield is deterministic** (Q1A). `curve` maps cumulative extraction to per-cycle yield; depletion is arithmetic, not a roll. **Variation comes from discrete campaign events** — accessibility change, equipment condition, collapse, licence suspension — each an attributed journaled act moving effective yield, never a per-period roll. A **survey reveals bands, never the exact reserve**, and the revealed band is per principal and is itself a recorded fact.

**Extraction** is scheduled, journaled **lot creation** on a deterministic key — doc 09 C8 already lists production and upkeep among bounded-idempotency-key actions (`09:1019-1028`, main). One cycle produces one `Lot` with `Lineage::Origin` at the extractor's endpoint (cut 2) and debits `reserve` in the same batch.

**Tenure lifecycle** (Q14A). A claim is **transferable**, **pledgeable** as collateral (`Encumbrance::Collateral`, `record.rs:537-542`, main), **contestable** through an E4 contract with a `Before` deadline and a named adjudicator (T3's shape), **suspendable** and **seizable** by the issuing authority. Overlapping political authority needs no fiction that an entitlement balance is land title: two governments may each issue a claim over one area, and `ControlKind::{DeJure, DeFacto}` (`mush-faction/src/data.rs:414-430`, main) says which is worth anything on the ground.

**Owner abandonment lands here** (T3; doc 10 §15; 08f §1.1 keeps it "in E7 with the mechanical recipe written out"): a unilateral `AssetEffect` setting `title_owner` to none with custody unchanged, a **direct attributed asset action** that need not masquerade as a bilateral contract — next to the claim seizure that needs the same unilateral title effect. The salvage claim that turns possession into title stays an ordinary contract with a `Before` contest deadline and a named adjudicator.

### 3.4 Cut 4 — `mush-industry`: facilities, recipes, work orders

This cut **is** the production-slice proposal, folded onto the binding decisions. Its §4 shapes, §5 clock semantics, §7 scenario and §8 E7a–E7d gates are adopted; four things change.

**Three overrides and one specification.** (1) **Ov — materials come from `mush-inventory`, not an asset container or "material vault"** (Q2D/Q13A) — `material_source: EndpointRef`. (2) **Ov — the value reservation is an E4 `Hold`, not an industry reservation**: T4 and doc 10 §15 keep holds money-only and forbid a resource field, while the proposal lists money, materials and capacity as undifferentiated "reservations". ### 3.6 Cut 6 — `mush-sim`: carrier journeys, promotion and demotion

**Journey semantics.** T1: cost and duration are deterministic functions of the `RouteDef` (cut 1), drive and payload, declared departure, propellant plan and the body-pair **alignment band on a campaign cadence** — no wall-clock randomness changes a contract after publication. **Promotion and demotion** (Q15A). *The governing rule: the system never runs tactically as a whole.* A domain promotes to `DomainClock::Pulse` on **four deterministic triggers** — a player character present in a contested context, a **credible contact** between opposed parties, an **interception** along a journey leg, or the **activation of a declared operation** (cut 9). **Why there is no `ContactRecord`** (08i answer 1, overriding 08i §6.1's "propagation registers its own family"). `mush-tactics` remains the **sole `ContactState` authority**; a second contact-lifecycle family would be two authoritative implementations of one migrated action, which 09 §7 forbids. Propagation owns the *ingress code* and the *phantom subjects*; the family, its validator (`ContactGuardValidator`) and its lifecycle rules are K's. The ingress is the trusted coordinator path — a `Registrar::schedule_source` adapter whose drafts enter `scheduled_action_ingress` under the `"scheduler"` principal class with deterministic keys, staged by `ActionCoordinator::prepare_and_stage_scheduled` — and every intent is **bounded** (`MAX_OBSERVATIONS_PER_PASS`, §7) and **deterministic** (same context, same set, same order). A cross-owner key is refused by `ActionTxn::checked_key`, which is what makes "propagation cannot forge tactics' state" a kernel property rather than a convention.

**One context, two resolvers — not one reachability step** (Q4A). They share geometry, medium, range, occlusion, interference and time-of-flight, and answer different questions. **Detection** resolves signature, emissions posture, medium, line of sight, sensor capability, noise floor and observer posture. **Delivery** resolves addressing, transmitter/receiver compatibility, routing, relays, encryption, jamming and store-and-forward. R4's two cases are *a jammed unit that still takes orders* and *a courier intercepted but never detected*. Contacts project as `PerceivedRef::Contact { provider, id, generation }` over the kernel's `ContactId` — produced by `mush-tactics`' `ContactState` family, which propagation *feeds* (above); Draft 0.2's "vocabulary with no producer until now" is struck. Unheld contacts already collapse to one unavailable result in `Engine::resolve_legal_action`, so the fog rule below starts from the kernel's behaviour, not against it.

**Contacts are local; sharing is a delivery** (Q16A). A transmitted report creates or updates the **recipient observer's own** `ContactState` entry — an `ObservationOp::Share` intent, i.e. tactics' `contact_share`, submitted only when the envelope carrying it is `Delivered` — with source, age, confidence and provenance on the side projection, so a stale relayed track is visibly stale and a false report is attributable. **There is no omniscient fused record anywhere.** **Unidentified** contacts exist from the start because the family already keeps truth private; **false** contacts exist from the start as propagation-owned phantoms (`SubjectKey::Scoped`), because retrofitting them would change every consumer's assumption that a contact has a subject. A **truthless** contact — one with no subject at all — is deferred to K's `ContactState` v2 and no E7 cut depends on it.

**Orders require delivery** (T15). Issuing revision *N* does not make it the recipient's active order. The recipient obeys the **newest valid revision it has actually received**; under ECM it continues its last order and fallback doctrine while the commander sees the replacement unacknowledged. On restored reachability, deliver the **newest valid superseding order**, not an obsolete backlog. **An acknowledgement is itself a delivery** that must reach the issuer (Q4A) — which is *why* the commander can see a replacement unacknowledged. Ordinary jamming blocks or delays; content corruption and deception require an explicit electronic-warfare result and are later.

**Cadence** (Q4A). Tactical propagation runs on the **domain tick** (cut 8). With no domain active, interplanetary mail, market reports and couriers run a **coarse scheduled delivery pass on a campaign cadence**.

**Commands.** `+contacts` · `+contact <id>` · `+order <recipient>=<order>` · `+orders` (issued, with envelope state) · `+orders/ack <id>` · `@prop/envelopes <subject>` · `@prop/pass` (staff).

one observer's record is invisible to another on the same side; a `False` contact renders identically to a real one; error messages do not disambiguate; **timing does not** (a detection miss costs the same work as a hit). *Provenance:* a relayed report carries source, age and `via`; revision 2 arriving after 3 is `Superseded` and never becomes active; a jammed recipient keeps its last delivered order and fallback doctrine; restored reachability delivers only the newest valid revision, not the backlog; expiry is idempotent. *Ack:* the issuer sees `Transmitted` until the ack itself arrives; an ack lost in transit leaves the order `Delivered` and the issuer uninformed — a probe asserts the commander's view says exactly that; a duplicate ack is a HIT. *Cadence:* with no domain active a courier resolves on the campaign pass; promoting a domain mid-flight moves that leg onto the domain tick without duplicating or dropping it. *Bounds:* `MAX_CONTACTS_PER_OBSERVER`, `CONTACT_TTL`, `ENVELOPE_TTL` and payload size refuse rather than grow. *Restart:* every envelope state hydrates; the delivery pass is **panic-safe and its bound is paged, not logged** — E4c's V6 silenced every economic event in a process after four panics inside a bounded observer, and a propagation pass has the same shape; an ack for a **superseded** revision does not mark the newest one acknowledged; a payload over the size cap is refused at issue, not truncated at delivery; every `Envelope`, `Phantom` and `ContactProvenance` record re-enters through its constructor (§4) and a stored set over `MAX_CONTACTS_PER_OBSERVER` refuses on load. *Added by Draft 0.3 (one contact authority):* a probe asserts **no E7 crate registers a `ContactState` family or a `Contact` guard validator** — `mush-tactics` is the only owner; **The frozen set of accepted intents is a window.** K's durable `ActionWindowRecord` (`crates/mush-core/src/action_window.rs`; 08i §2 row 8) is the per-tick intent set: `checked_submit` accepts one intent per `(actor, truth_subject)` slot (`WindowIntentSlot`) and a resubmission **replaces** it — which is S2A's latest-order-wins for free — `resolution_order` yields the canonical order the resolver consumes, and `checked_consume` closes the window in the tick's own commit. `MAX_WINDOW_CAPACITY = 256` **is** `MAX_INTENTS_PER_TICK` (§7); cut 8 defines no second constant.

**Resolution.** The pulse resolver does **not** create one K action per NPC, weapon or unit. At `MAX_UNITS_PER_DOMAIN` = 64 the write count is ~6 % of the replay-write cap, so **64 is a latency and CPU policy figure, not a K3-derived one** — the K3 caps are a hard ceiling far above it. §9 q6's measurement must report which of the three is nearest, and the pinning test fails when any of them passes 60 %. **Measured, never assumed** (08i answer 15): a tick that writes one post-image per unit for `MAX_UNITS_PER_DOMAIN` units, its window consume, its `ResolutionRecord` and its boundary facts is *encoded* and checked against `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_REPLAY_WRITES` (1,024) and `MAX_AUTHORITATIVE_BUNDLE_BYTES` (16 MiB) in a pinned test; the store's error shapes carry the cap, not the measured size, so the measurement is E7's to make (08i §2 row 12). ### 3.9 Cut 9 — `mush-campaign`: declared operations, formations, resolution

R1 is satisfied by the shared id and the one-to-one link without conflating consent with runtime. 10a's 5A is rejected because it re-learns the E4b2 lesson: every roster edit becomes either a terms amendment or a hole in the hash.

```rust
struct OperationTerms {                      // FROZEN at activation (T6)
    operation: OperationId, objective: ObjectiveSpec, legal_targets: Vec<TargetClass>,
    stakes: Vec<Consequence>, bond: HoldId, sides: Vec<SideSpec>,

                       composition: Composition, command_roles: Vec<RoleSpec> }
```

**Terms freeze; the roster does not touch them.** **Changing the roster never changes the terms hash** — a reinforcement is not an amendment to what the defenders consented to be told, and `consent_intact` (`contract.rs:1066-1068`, main) is re-checked at every transition.

**How the hash is actually shared, because Draft 0.1's "share the helper" is not buildable as written.** `consent_hash(issuer: &PrincipalId, consent: &ConsentSurface) -> [u8;32]` (`contract.rs:1014`, main) takes an **economy** `ConsentSurface` — parties, `ContractTerms`, conditions, deadline, visibility, scope, adjudicator, evidence — and its body is *sort the parties by `principal_key`, build a field-ordered canonical struct, `serde_json::to_vec`, `Sha256::digest`* (`:1015-1029`). `mush-campaign` cannot call it without either taking a `mush-economy` dependency **and** stuffing seat/operation fields into contract-shaped ones, or forking the discipline — and Q10D/10a §6 sharpening 3 forbid the fork. **The buildable reading of "share, not fork":** `mush-economy` factors the last three lines out as `pub fn consent_digest(domain: &str, canonical: &impl Serialize) -> [u8;32]`, `consent_hash` becomes a two-line wrapper over it with `domain = "contract.consent.v1"`, and `OperationTerms` and cut 10's `SeatOffer` call it with `"campaign.operation.v1"` / `"campaign.seat.v1"` over **their own** field-ordered canonical structs. One discipline, one implementation, **domain-separated** so an operation hash can never be replayed as a contract hash — which forking would not have given either. Sorting rules carry over verbatim: any principal vector sorts by `principal_key`, every other vector's order *is* consented content. This is a small, additive `mush-economy` change and belongs in §10's handbacks.

**T6's visibility classes.** `EconomicVisibility::Audience { members, announce_issuer }` with `announce_issuer` defaulting to `false` (`record.rs:339-365`, `:362-363`, main) is already T6's *"notice of the window, not the plan"*: public; eligible defenders; participating organizations; covert existence with a visible vulnerability warning; staff/audit only. **"Declared" means every party entitled to defend receives sufficient notice and knows the window — not that the attacker's plan or identity is published.** `MAX_AUDIENCE` is 32 (`record.rs:322-323`).

**The bond** is an economy hold placed by the attacker's **own** authority — never an industry reservation, because it is money (T4). S5A's ≈ 24–72 h notice is `OPERATION_NOTICE_MIN/MAX` (§7).

**Formations** (Q9A/T8/R1/R2). `composition` is a **constraint set, not one enum**, and **`exact` is `minimum == maximum`**, not a separate mode. **Strictness is scoped** — at *creation*, at *doctrine qualification* ("is this still a full squadron?"), or as *an operation's requirement* — **never to a formation's existence or its ability to deploy**. An understrength permanent formation is normal, not an error. `ruleset-tacsim` authors the instances in cut 1's packs, so a four-unit lance and a five-unit star share an echelon and remain distinct kinds. **Each node names exactly one owning `OrgScopeId`** and `force.*` capabilities resolve through the existing grant machinery (`crates/mush-org/src/authorize.rs:143-200`, main), so **R2 needs no core change**. It does need one composition-root addition: `org_capability_vocabulary()` is **non-empty** and is a typo catcher at the grant command (`crates/mush-server/src/modules.rs`; at `a1934a7` it holds the economy ids, `mush_housing::CAPABILITIES`, `org.role.manage` and `org.grant.delegate` — §4.0; `crates/mush-org/src/wiring.rs:78-93`), so granting an unlisted `force.*` id **fails loudly at `@org/grant`** — the ids must be added there. Administrative parentage is unchanged by attachment; **a unit is in at most one operation at a time** (R1), with exactly one operational command parent within it.

**Terminal settlement** (Q17A). An operation ends by writing **one** `campaign.operation.resolved` root fact into the mechanical journal (T2). Every consequence is a separate, **idempotent**, causally linked consumer transition keyed off that root. `Resolving { pending }` is only meaningful if the set is closed, so **`ConsumerId` is a closed engine enum, enumerated here**:

```rust
enum ConsumerId { CampaignOutcome,   // the outcome row recorded on the operation           — always
                  FrontControl,      // `mush-faction` Front/ControlRecord (lib.rs:235-274) — iff a
                                     //   Consequence names a (RegionId, ControlKind)
                  BondSettlement,    // the E4 Hold released, forfeited or split            — iff bond
                  ContractOutcomes,  // outcome selection on linked contracts (contract.rs:227-276)
                                     //                                                     — iff any
                  ForceRetirement }  // task force dissolved, units released                — always
```

The **required set is computed once, at entry to `Resolving`, from the frozen terms**, and stored on the runtime — never re-derived later. That is the whole crash-safety argument and it has a second edge Draft 0.1 missed: if a later build adds a sixth consumer, **a stored `Resolving` set must not grow and a `Resolved` operation must never re-open**; an unknown stored `ConsumerId` at boot fails closed and pages level 2 rather than being dropped as unrecognised. The operation stays `Resolving` until every member acknowledges, and only then reaches `Resolved`. 10a's 17B is rejected: it claims an atomicity the tree does not have across three crates (08c §12).

**Commands.** `@op/declare <objective>` · `@op/terms <id>/<field>=<value>` (pre-activation only) · `@op/activate <id>` · `@op/join <id>=<unit|formation>` · `@op/withdraw <id>=<unit>` · `@op/show <id>` (per audience) · `@op/resolve <id>=<outcome>` · `@op/consumers <id>` (staff) · `@force/create|attach|detach|assign` · `@formation/show <id>`.

**Tests (~30).** *Freeze:* a terms edit after activation refuses; a roster edit leaves `consent_hash` byte-identical; a mutated surface makes `consent_intact` false and the operation refuses to progress — it disputes and pages rather than settling; the notice window sits inside `OPERATION_NOTICE_MIN/MAX`. *Audience:* a non-audience viewer sees no operation id, issuer or objective (the existence-hiding renderer treats it like an account id); an audience member sees the window and notice but **not** the plan when `announce_issuer` is false; a recursive leak check finds no covert operation's existence; an audience over `MAX_AUDIENCE` refuses. *One operation:* a unit already in an operation refuses a second; withdrawal releases it; a formation attached keeps its administrative parent; a node with no `OrgScopeId` is unconstructible; a `force.*` capability missing from the vocabulary fails at registration, not silently at use. *Composition:* an understrength formation exists and deploys; the same constraint set refuses at creation and at doctrine qualification; `minimum == maximum` behaves as `exact`; a lance and a star share an echelon and stay distinct kinds. *Bond:* the bond is a `Hold` placed by the attacker's own authority; an attacker without funds cannot activate; it settles exactly once per outcome row. *Resolution:* the root fact is written once; each consumer transition is idempotent under replay; a crash after two of five consumers resumes and does not double-pay; `Resolved` is unreachable while any consumer is pending; a permanently failing consumer opens a level-2 escalation with the recommended command filled in (08c §4.6) and **suspends only the operation**. *Fronts:* a resolution advancing a front writes one `ControlRecord` change, and re-running the consumer writes none — and because `mush-faction`'s `Clock` is a **segment counter with no due instant** (`sim.rs:67-98`, main), the consumer fills segments and never restamps a deadline. *Added by the review:* one operation's permanently failing consumer **does not starve another operation's fan-out** (08c §5.2 B4's wall-of-disputes shape); a **bond released twice across a restart** is unreachable — the release is keyed off the root fact and is a HIT on replay; a stored `Resolving` set containing an id this build does not know **pages rather than completing**; adding a consumer in a later build leaves already-`Resolved` operations untouched (a probe re-boots with a sixth id registered); `@op/show` for a non-audience viewer is byte-identical for a covert operation and for an id that never existed, at every verbosity, and `@op/consumers` is staff-only; a `force.*` capability granted **before** it was added to the vocabulary still authorizes nothing (`authorize` matches by equality, `authorize.rs:143-200`) and `@org/show` flags it rather than deleting it; every operation, terms, runtime and formation record re-enters through its constructor (§4), and a stored participant list over `MAX_OPERATION_PARTICIPANTS` refuses on load.

**Verify lenses.** **Z (freeze integrity)** — can any path change what a defender consented to be told, or progress an operation whose surface no longer hashes? **A (audience)** — can a viewer learn a covert operation exists, or its plan, through any render or error? **I (idempotent fan-out)** — can a crash, retry or duplicate root fact double-apply a consequence, or strand an operation in `Resolving`?

**Hands to cut 10:** the operation scope seat grants, musters and readiness obligations attach to, and the participation runtime settlement indexes on.

### 3.10 Cut 10 — crew, proxy seats, musters, the oracle

**Crew** (C11A/C13A/C14A/C15A/T16). Named NPC objects occupy dramatically relevant positions — captain, helm, tactical, engineering, marine command; routine watchstanders, technicians, loaders, stewards and medical staff are an **aggregate complement** carrying readiness, capacity and morale. A **periodic readiness obligation** covers ordinary payroll and support — an E4b1 `Obligation` on the **operation cadence with success notifications off** (doc 10 §15) — and individual employment contracts are used only when a named NPC's personal terms matter. **T16:** the actor at a station supplies the relevant traits, and **a human proxy directing an NPC still uses the NPC's sheet** — the human adds judgment, not an absent character's stats. Any wider effect needs a causal action or changed ship state, **never a free global bonus for seat occupancy**. Crew quarters reuse E5a's tenancy shape (08d §3.5), not a new one.

**Proxy seats are consent grants, not contracts** (Q10D; doc 10 R6 **as amended**), and their `consent_hash` uses cut 9's shared `consent_digest` under the `"campaign.seat.v1"` domain — never a forked hasher, never an economy `ConsentSurface` stuffed with seat fields (§3.9). The governing fact: **an OOC proxy player is not an in-world principal.** C1A/R3 forbid an account principal and keep it out of any contract or canon record, and T13 says the absent character did not take the station — **so an E4 contract can name neither, and there is no third candidate.** 10a's 10A is rejected for exactly that reason.

```rust
struct SeatOffer { id: SeatOfferId, operation: OperationId, audience: AudienceSpec,
                   eligibility: EligibilitySpec, expires: ClockStamp, scope: SeatScope,
                   revocation: RevocationTerms, fallback: FallbackSpec, consent_hash: [u8; 32] }

                   mission_order: OrderRef, roe: RoeRef, permitted_area: AreaRef }
struct SeatGrant { offer: SeatOfferId, actor: ObjId /* the NPC */, driver: DriverToken,
                   accepted_at: ClockStamp, consent_hash: [u8; 32], state: GrantState }

```

**T11's guardrails are the granted channel set on the NPC's `StationSpec`** (`mush-sim/src/data.rs:414-435`, channels at `:426`, main), **not contract prose**. Proxy control grants tactical discretion, **not** title, administration or an unrestricted possession verb: no asset transfer or abandonment, scuttling, fallback change, loadout or crew mutation, organization spending, surrender of unrelated assets, departure from the authorized operation, or assumption of another station. **Ordinary tactical loss cannot be made impossible without making the seat fictitious.**

**Live control is a lease derived from the grant, not the grant itself — and the lease is K6's `ControlLeaseState`.** `Dormant → Active` is a `control_handoff_request` through `crates/mush-sim/src/handoff.rs` (a private module; `HandoffProvider`/`HandoffScheduleAdapter` are not re-exported) naming the grant's holder, a `reason` (`seat.activate`) and the safe `boundary`, applied by `checked_apply_handoff` under the scheduler principal; `Active { epoch }` records the lease epoch that apply returned. Revocation bumps the unit's control epoch immediately (`bump_control_gen`, `crates/mush-sim/src/data.rs`) so stale intents fail `channel_grant_is_current`, and the seat returns to its declared fallback at a safe boundary (T11) through the same request path. **Cut 10 persists no lease-like record of its own** and reads the lease family for every "is this channel held" question (08i §4).

**The K6 gate, as a predicate over the K6 memo rather than a promise** (08i answer 2; §2.4). Draft 0.2's three-condition pre-K6 predicate assumed the lease kernel was absent; it is present, so condition (2) — *no live domain* — is retired in favour of the memo predicate below. **Draft 0.3 also retired condition (3) and that was wrong** (08j S1): it rested the "no channel under a live lease" refusal on `ControlLeaseState::checked_request_handoff`, which does not perform it. `checked_request_handoff` refuses on `require_revision`, `DuplicatePending`/`PendingExists`, `self.holder == draft.next_holder` → `NoControlChange`, and `require_boundary`; it never compares `draft.requested_by` to `self.holder`, because **requesting over a live lease is the ordinary path** — `mush_contract_tests::tactical_gate`'s Gate-3 revoke and the `Change::ControlLease` mutator in `crates/mush-server/src/world_task.rs` both unwrap it, and `HandoffProvider::prepare_request` (`crates/mush-sim/src/handoff.rs`) adds no holder check and registers no `AuthorityGuard`. As written, cut 10 would have evicted a live human operator and the gate's middle leg would have failed.

**The gate is therefore two policy refusals plus one kernel boundary refusal**, over one memo condition:

1. *Policy (Draft 0.2's condition (1)):* the target station is **vacant or held by the grant's own declared `FallbackSpec`** — a live human operator is **never** evicted by a seat activation; the request is refused naming the holder's station and nothing else about the holder. A test pins that **E7 ships no eviction path at all.** Cut 10 writes this; nothing in the tree performs it today (no `SeatOffer`/`SeatGrant`/`FallbackSpec` symbol exists under `crates/*/src`, and `@spatial/unassign` + `@spatial/assign` in `crates/mush-sim/src/commands.rs` are themselves an eviction pair).
2. *Policy (Draft 0.2's condition (3), restored):* **no channel in the grant's `SeatScope.channels` is under a live lease held by anyone but the grant's own fallback.** Cut 10 asks the lease family — it persists no lease-like record of its own (08i §4) — and refuses naming the station. This is **cut 10's own check**: the kernel answers only the boundary.
3. *Kernel (the only kernel-answered leg):* a handoff whose safe `boundary` has not arrived is not applied — `checked_apply_handoff` → `ControlLeaseError::BeforeBoundary`.

*Memo condition (retiring Draft 0.2's condition (2)):* activation of a seat on a unit **inside a `Pulse` domain** requires the K6 memo's rows that cut 10 depends on to read **production** — *control leases/handoffs* (production kernel and `mush-sim` authority; the policy ingress is cut 5's) and *contacts* (production family; the sensor ingress is cut 7's) — **plus cut 6's placement and cut 8's unit migrations landed**, which the cut order guarantees. The memo's *fixture-only* rows — atomic tactical damage, unit DTOs, bridges, projection-leak proofs — are not on cut 10's path: damage is cut 8's, DTOs are `unit.status`'s projection, and the leak proofs are cut 7/8 probes. **The memo now exists** — `docs/design/09a-k6-look-back.md` (`7e1cf46`) — so the predicate reads *it*, not 08i answer 2's paraphrase, and cut 10's `#[ignore]` pin cites the memo's rows by name.

**LLM-driven seats are K7-gated** (08i answer 3): a proxy seat whose driver is an LLM controller needs K7.0–K7.3's `PendingDecision` lifecycle; cuts 1–11 ship deterministic fallback drivers only, and the LLM rows in the tests below are `#[ignore]` pins naming K7. An **E4 contract links to the operation only when an actual fictional principal** — embodied character, named NPC, organization — **has economic terms**; an unpaid proxy seat needs none, and inventing one to hold the consent hash is 10a's 10A error.

**Attribution and audit** (Q19A/T9/R7). The mechanical journal records the **in-world actor** and an **opaque decision-driver token**, and nothing else. A **server-side, audit-only** record links that token to the account; ordinary projections, renders and exports cannot reverse it, and one staff verb can. OOC participation history and matchmaking standing **consume the audit record**, live outside the world, and **never become IC settlement**: T13's absent character earns **no** pay, advancement, reputation, knowledge or property, and standing is a **crew-call eligibility policy input only** (R6).

**Focus** (Q20A/T12). OOC proxy control **consumes the account's one foreground focus** (cut 5). The player's own character remains at their durable location and **cannot act concurrently**; entering proxy mode hands off that character's station, clearly identifies the represented NPC and exposes **only that NPC station's observer projection** (cut 7). Leaving restores AI control; it never moves or protects the player's character, who is present and vulnerable under E5's attention budget and non-response semantics (C22A/R5). **Accepting an offer does not seize focus** — the grant is `Dormant` until activated.

**Musters** (T14/Q21A). E7 uses **E6a's `mush-party`** — game-neutral, `{mush-core}` deps only, not a principal, never learning about money (08f §2). **There is never a TacSim-specific party record**; if E7 lands first it pulls that exact cut forward unchanged. An operation publishes start, expected duration, committed formations, character billets, proxy-eligible NPC stations, certifications and restrictions, command succession, secrecy and compensation. At muster a slot resolves to a player character, a human-directed NPC, an AI-controlled named NPC, automation, or vacancy. **Missing players reduce discretion and resilience but do not cancel a valid operation.** Late arrival and early departure use the same explicit handoff (C29A).

**The distinctness oracle's consumers** (Q11A/R3/C23A/C24A). Roster lock and side assignment call `all_accounts_distinct` and **store only the verdict**; the private account id never enters a journal, contract or canon record. An account may materially assist **only one side** of a defined operation, battle, claim contest or similar competitive episode.

**Participation facts** (R6/T13; doc 10 §15). Settlement splits index on **participation**, which E4b2's outcome-indexed terms already support once the sim emits `sim.station.served { actor, station, from, to }`, answered from an **applied marker, never a status** (08c §6.2; 08f §7 item 3). Kill, salvage and delivery credit are the same shape.

**Commands.** `+seat/offer <operation>/<unit>/<station>` · `+seat/list` · `+seat/accept <id>` · `+seat/decline <id>` · `+seat/activate <id>` · `+seat/leave` · `@seat/revoke <id>` · `+muster <operation>` · `+crewcall/list|apply` · `@crew/complement <unit>` · `@crew/readiness <unit>` · `@seat/audit <token>` (staff only).

**Tests (~30).** *Guardrails:* each of T11's nine forbidden acts refuses from a proxy seat and succeeds from the owner; a granted channel set narrower than the station's limits the proxy exactly; a proxy cannot assume a second station; an intent outside `permitted_area` refuses; **ordinary tactical loss is permitted** — a probe asserts the seat is not made safe. *Revocation:* revocation bumps the epoch and every queued human, NPC, pathfinder and LLM intent under the old grant fails stale; the seat returns to its declared fallback **at a safe boundary**, not mid-tick; a disconnect uses the same path; a revoked grant cannot be re-activated. *K6 gate:* activating a grant over a **live** human operator refuses naming the station, and a test pins that E7 ships no eviction path; activation is a `control_handoff_request` whose apply waits for the `boundary` (a probe asserts no `Active` state before `checked_apply_handoff` returns); a request over a channel leased to another holder is refused by **cut 10's own policy check** — `checked_request_handoff` never asks who holds the lease (08j S1), so a probe also asserts the kernel call alone does *not* refuse it. *Focus:* activating a seat releases the player's own station's control and fires its fallback; the player's character stays where it was, present and vulnerable, and cannot act; leaving restores AI control and does not move the character; accepting without activating seizes nothing. *Attribution:* the journal carries actor + driver token and **never** an account id (a recursive scan of journal bytes asserts it); the audit record resolves the token for staff and nobody else; an export cannot reverse it. *Absent-player settlement:* the absent character receives no pay, advancement, reputation, knowledge or property from a proxy sortie; canon attaches to the NPC, unit, organization and operation; matchmaking standing changes and **cannot** be spent on character power. *Distinctness:* two characters on one account fail `all_accounts_distinct` at roster lock and side assignment; an oracle answering `None` fails closed; only the verdict is stored. *Musters:* a slot resolving to vacancy does not cancel the operation; a late arrival takes a seat through the same handoff; a probe asserts no TacSim party type exists. *Readiness:* the obligation runs on the operation cadence, is silent on success, and its arrears follow E4b1's ladder. *Added by the review:* **the gate is tested as three independent refusals — two policy, one kernel** — a station held by another human actor (cut-10 policy), a channel leased to another holder (cut-10 policy; the kernel does not refuse it), and an apply before the boundary (`checked_apply_handoff` refuses) each refuse alone, and a seat on a unit in a `Pulse` domain activates **only** when the memo predicate reads production and cuts 6/8 are present (the `#[ignore]` pin names the memo); an LLM-driven seat is an `#[ignore]` pin naming K7; a `Dormant` grant on an operation that **aborts** expires rather than surviving to a later operation; a `DriverToken` is **per grant**, so the same player accepting two seats produces two tokens and neither correlates them in any projection; a grant whose **audit row is missing at boot** refuses activation and pages, it does not activate un-attributed; `+seat/list` shows nothing about an operation the viewer is not in the audience of, and a declined offer leaves no trace a third party can read; **Verify lenses.** **G (guardrail escape)** — can a proxy reach any authority outside its channel set, area or operation? **R (revocation and boundary)** — can control persist past a revocation, be lost mid-tick, or be handed off before the boundary? **A (audit irreversibility)** — can the account behind a driver token be recovered by anything but the staff verb, or reach a journal, contract or canon record?

**Hands to cut 11:** a crewed, operationally committed force the proving loop can run, and the participation facts settlement indexes on.

### 3.11 Cut 11 — the recovery provider, the corridor seed pack, the rate policy

**The recovery provider** (Q7A/T7; doc 10 S3A). **No new engine machinery.** The provider is a **government or corporate organization principal** owning the loaner inventory, accounts, contracts and policy. A system faucet may **subsidize** it under the `[economy]` mint cap via `create_system_obligation` (`crates/mush-economy/src/api.rs:1105-1203`, main) — **a `System` principal can never itself be a contract party** (08f D-E6-4, which the tree forces). **Eligibility is a deterministic predicate over recorded facts examining accessible operational assets** — unencumbered, reachable, deployable — **rather than total title**, so a player whose only hull is an unreachable wreck still qualifies. **Staff are never the gate.** The grant is a contract placing `Encumbrance::Lease` on the loaner (`record.rs:528-536`, main), with upkeep an `Obligation` on the campaign cadence and restrictions as ordinary conditions. Q7A requires the plan to state five things, and it does:

| Question (Q7A) | Answer |
|---|---|
| Pool exhaustion | the queue holds; the provider stops granting and pages **level 1** — an empty pool is not a decision |
| Queue priority | first-eligible, then longest-waiting; ties by claim time |
| Per-player cooldown | `RECOVERY_COOLDOWN` after a lease ends, waived when the loss was inside the restrictions |
| Insurance | approved operations only; outside them the lease is uninsured and a loss becomes a debt |
| Replenishment | the provider's own production work orders (cut 4) and salvage recovery, funded by its budget — **never a mint into the pool** |
| Upgrade / loss | buy out at a declared price or re-lease one tier up; loss inside the restrictions is written off, outside it becomes a debt |

**The corridor seed pack** (Q6A/Q18A/doc 10 §11). The full Mars–Phobos–Ceres content as **data**: propulsion and route tables, the ten commodity families, component definitions, recipes, formation kinds, combat formulas, insurance and warfare policy in **rules-definition packs**; the corridor locations, the player corporation, the government faction and its rival, the refinery, the mining drones, the hauler, the patrol craft, the dropship, the infantry, the tank and the mech in a **campaign-seed pack**. **No hot mutation** (Q18A): a change publishes a new version that coexists, and live units, blueprints, recipes and open contracts stay **pinned** until an explicit migration or in-world refit — **also T1's guarantee that a published hauling contract's route assumptions cannot shift underneath it.** **The sale path is an ordinary E4b2 contract** between the seller and a funded NPC corporation principal — consideration in `InstrumentKind::{Currency, Commodity}`, a `Before` deadline, the physical hand-over an attributed cut-2 inventory act linked back by `ExternalRef` and gated by an `inventory.transferred` fact (D-E7-31). No listing, no quote, no vendor record, no order book: all four are E6c's (§1.1).

**The timeline's rate policy** (Q8D). **The kernel owns generations** (K4a, §2.1); **the campaign layer sets rate policy** — doc 10 S1A's ≈ one OOC hour per IC day is a **policy value, not a constant in code**. Every subsystem registers a **derived cadence adapter** over the one timeline. **Multiple named cadences are fine; multiple incompatible real→IC mappings are the danger** — WoD's and Shadowrun's clocks stay separate `CadenceId`s and never derive TacSim's rate, so a compression change cannot re-price a Shadowrun lifestyle (doc 10 §20). `catch_up_cap` stays **obligation-sweep policy** (`mush-economy/src/clock.rs:240-241`, `:264-295`, main); E7 gives **journeys, production, fronts and tactical domains each an explicit catch-up policy**, because they are not obligations:

| Subsystem | Catch-up policy on a new generation | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Journeys | **restamp preserving remaining IC duration** — journey clocks never pause | S6A |
| Production | **pause as `Interrupted { TimelineChanged }`** unless the campaign registers a restamp | proposal §5; D-E7-8 |

## 5. Kernel migration — a later cut, "E7k"

The proposal's **E7k** milestone is retained as E7's twelfth, later cut. It does **not** ship with cuts 1–11 and **nothing in them blocks on it** (10a §4 dep 2). Also deferred to it, and named here so it is not forgotten: cut 9's `consent_digest` factoring (§3.9) is *not* part of E7k — it is a small additive `mush-economy` change that ships **with cut 9** (§10).

**The gate, with owners** (08i answer 13, G-B; §2.4). **All three §2.4 gates are now closed** (re-read on the merged main, 2026-09-05; 08j §4). Gate 2 was closed on `91208ab` for commands, ticks, boot callbacks, observers and contextual hooks (`Ctx::action_state`/`scan_action_state`), and its one residue — no typed read inside `move_attempt`, `carry_attempt`, rendering or perception hooks (G-B) — closed with K7's `HookReadContext` (`0e3f2e8`), which `crates/mush-housing/src/hooks.rs` `AccessHook` already takes. Gates 1 and 3 closed with **K8.0** (`3d0788d`): `ReplayWrite::LedgerChunkSet` with `ReplayTarget::Module` routing and both backends normalizing it, and canonically derived `ledger_seals` inside the bundle — with the **legacy-predecessor bar surviving on purpose** (`flush_prepared_action` still rejects an unresolved legacy ledger predecessor), so a migration commit moves a whole path or none of it. 08i row 26's E fallback draft is withdrawn. **E7k's remaining precondition is the K8 harness alone** — K8.1–K8.3, which 09 §K8 marks closed together with K8.0 and the CI jobs, so in practice the gate that remains is not a K item at all. **Then a migration review opens.** Q3A is explicit — *the trigger opens a review, never an automatic migration* — and 08c §12 says of the equivalent move that it "is then still an explicit **owner decision**, not a mechanical port" (`08c:1806-1808`, main), **because the journal would stop being sole authority for money-adjacent truth, which is the property every economy invariant is anchored to.**

**What would move — re-scoped** (08j S11). Draft 0.2 listed "the cut-2 inventory reservations and cut-4 capacity reservations **from `mush-inventory`/`mush-industry` documents** onto authoritative K3 state families". That contradicted this plan's own §4.1: cut 2's and cut 4's reservations are **authoritative from the first commit**, on K4b's `ReservationBook` (`crates/mush-core/src/resource.rs`), with per-owner `AuthorityGuardKind::Reservation` validators — they are never conventional documents, so there is nothing there for E7k to move. K8.5's lane rule (09b §2) forecloses the alternative reading outright. What E7k actually moves is therefore **three things**: (i) **the Ledger append accompanying a settlement**, onto `PreparedLedgerAppend::authoritative_replay_write` inside the bundle; (ii) **work-order transitions** (cut 4's `LegacyPostImage` workflow documents, which name their future family at registration); and (iii) **cross-owner output materialization** — title + custody + placement + inventory in one bundle. Cut 9's resolution fan-out is a candidate too, and Draft 0.2's caveat — "only when the kernel provides a truly atomic mixed bundle" (Q17A) — **is now satisfied** by `ReplayWrite::LedgerChunkSet`, so the fan-out's obstacle is scope and review, not a missing primitive.

**Until then** the economy journal remains authority and E7's conventional documents are projections or workflow records. **Every record in cuts 2–4 and 9 is shaped for a mechanical migration** (proposal §2.3, adopted verbatim as a build rule): canonical whole-document post-images, one mutation per key per transition, explicit revisions, deterministic idempotency keys, and no hidden side effects after the final fallible preflight.

**What must prove equivalence before the conventional handler is removed** (proposal §8) — the **memory and Redis restart matrix** and the **acknowledgement-loss matrix**, both against both stores: restart after commit / before RAM install loads the committed post-state; restart after ack / before output recovers the receipt and keyframe; a lost ownership ack converges by replaying the same request UID; acknowledgement loss cannot duplicate state or re-run RNG; an action-created object survives allocator restart **without id reuse**; a partial materialization always has a complete exact pending intent; legacy pre-state dependencies cannot be lost beneath a later committed action (doc 09 K3 exit criteria, `09:2122-2133` *(K)*, under `#### K3.1. Durability-audit closure` at `09:2051`). Redis-gated tests are explicit ignored tests locally **and** an explicit Linux CI job, so skipped coverage cannot count as passing (K3.1e, `09:2071-2078` *(K)*).
