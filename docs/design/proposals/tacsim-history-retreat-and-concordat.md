## 1. The brief

The owner's framing, restated: a single government on Earth is laughable *unless* the three
centuries between now and 2350 are dramatic enough to make it the least bad outcome. The climate
catastrophe supplies the drama, and does three more jobs at once. It explains why the 1950–2050
innovation curve did not hold from 2050 to 2350 — humanity had bigger problems. It avoids naming
cultural or national winners, because the coasts that held most of the world's population and
capital are the coasts that went. And it gives the Concord a *recent* founding — about eighty years
ago, within living memory — from which it launched a renaissance that reached the solar system on
technology refined in one of the pockets that kept moving during the interval. A mini dark age with
no loss of technology, then a release.

## 2. The argument, in four moves

Each of the owner's four claims is defensible; each gets stronger with one adjustment.

1. **Not a government: a concordat.** The Concord is a treaty polity that pools five functions —
   climate engineering and the adaptation fund, migration, orbital access and the fleet, the
   Registry, and currency clearing — and leaves internal governance to its member blocs. It formed
   because the old sovereignties were discredited by a shared existential problem, and because one
   party, the lunar habitats, held the only door to the frontier. "Legislature on Earth; nobody in
   the Belt cares which city" is already the right register. The name comes from the *Concordat*.
2. **Two causes for the flat curve, not one.** The catastrophe consumed the surplus and the
   institutions that fund big science. But the twentieth century's curve was also an S-curve: the
   cheap physics had been picked, and the next steps needed capital and stability that did not
   exist. Two reasons are more defensible than one, and together they explain both the stall and
   the speed of the later release.
3. **Name what was lost.** "No loss of technology" holds if the loss is *scale*, not knowledge.
   Digital archives are cheap, and every Enclave and habitat had reason to keep them. What went was
   the global supply web, the leading-edge fabs, and the budgets — which is why compute became
   scarce, and why automation in 2350 is excellent but never in charge (doc 10 §3's tone, given a
   cause).
4. **The pocket was Luna.** The lunar and orbital habitats, founded before the Retreat by agencies
   and consortia, had to master closed-loop ecology, regolith industry and compact fusion to keep
   breathing. Over a century they refined fusion propulsion and the entangled-pair foundry (doc 15).
   That is why the Concord's seat, Registry, clearing, yards, foundry and navy are all lunar, and
   why joining the Concordat was the only way for an Earth bloc to reach the new frontier.

## 4. The pockets, by type — never by nation

The owner's constraint is to project no cultural or identity winners. The history does that by
describing the pockets by geography and function only, and by making migration the reason every
corridor name is a mixture.

| Pocket | What kept it alive | What it refined | Where it is in 2350 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The lunar and orbital habitats** | Closed-loop necessity: no fossil alternative, no rescue | Closed-loop ecology, regolith industry, compact fusion; later fusion propulsion and the entangled-pair foundry | The Concord's seat, Registry, clearing, yards, foundry and navy. Tycho Mutual. |
| **The highland biotech Enclaves** | Capital, altitude, walls | Longevity, crop engineering, medicine | The Concord's biotech export; several are now Concord member blocs; their money is "dry money" |
| **The inland fission-and-hydro basins** | Power that did not depend on coasts or trade | Heavy industry, materials | The Concord's high-end manufacturing base; the population that reconstituted the blocs |
| **The archives** | Universities in exile, habitat libraries, Enclave data vaults | Nothing new; everything old | Why there was no loss of knowledge — only of scale |

What no pocket kept: leading-edge semiconductor fabrication and the supply web behind it. Compute
was scarce for a century; the surviving compute went to climate modelling, life support and
Enclave security. The AI curve flattened for lack of substrate, not for lack of ideas, and the
Charter's human-command clause was written by people who had lived a century on rationed compute.

## 5. The Concordat: what was pooled and what was not

Pooled — the five functions the Retreat proved needed pooling:

1. **Climate engineering and the adaptation fund.** Sea level keeps rising for centuries after
   warming stops. The fund is the Concord's largest standing commitment in 2350 and the reason its
   treasury is "large" and unavailable.
2. **Migration.** The one sovereign function no bloc could exercise alone.
3. **Orbital access and the fleet.** Luna's contribution and Luna's price.
4. **The Registry.** Already lunar, already the only trusted ledger; made the legal root of every
   claim off Earth.
5. **Currency clearing.** The credit, cleared through Luna — which is why Ceres Clearing and the
   Republic both owe Luna.

Not pooled: internal governance, law, taxation beyond the assessed contribution, culture. The blocs
remain blocs; the former Enclaves that were large enough sit in the legislature beside the popular
polities that reconstituted around them. The class polarization of the Retreat is therefore *inside*
the Concord's own house, not merely between the Concord and the corridor.

## 7. Defensibility: the objections and the answers

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Coasts can be defended; low countries exist." | Some were, and are. The losses came where subsidence, storms, poverty and scale coincided, and the mechanism was financial before it was hydrological: insurance, then credit, then people. Say "every coastal megacity abandoned, defended or rebuilt inland," never "the coasts are gone." |
| "Multi-metre sea-level rise by 2200 is the high end." | It is, and the story does not need it. One to two metres plus storm intensification and subsidence already empties the megacities by the 2120s; the multi-metre tail is what keeps the adaptation bill alive in 2350. |
| "A world government is implausible." | It is not a government. It is a concordat pooling five functions, joined because Luna held the only door to the frontier. Post-catastrophe federations form when the old sovereignties are discredited and the coordination problem is existential. |
| "Dark ages lose technology." | This one loses scale and capital, not knowledge: digital archives, self-interested Enclaves, and habitats that had to keep engineering alive to breathe. The named losses — fabs, the supply web, big-science budgets — are also why AI stayed a tool. |
| "Why did innovation resume so fast in 2270?" | It did not start from zero. The pockets had refined for a century; the Concordat added capital, people and a mandate. A renaissance is a release, not an invention. |
| "Why does it stall again by 2350?" | Sea level kept rising; the adaptation bill never ended; the Outbound's dividends went to Mars and the Belt, who then left. Late-institutional exhaustion is the ordinary fate of a founding generation's project. |
| "Why is the capital on the Moon?" | The habitats' price; the Registry and clearing were already there; and a seat off Earth is the outbound mandate made visible. |
| "Why do all the names look mixed?" | Everyone moved. The Retreat's migrations are why no corridor name signals a nation — the owner's goal, achieved by history rather than by omission. |
| "So who won?" | Nobody, by design. The pockets are typed by geography and function. No nation, language or culture is named as surviving, dominating or vanishing. |

## 8. Automation: why humans are still in the loop

The short answer: the AI of the 2030s and 2040s killed no one directly. It made the people who
owned it powerful enough to abandon everyone else, and then the world that fed it fell apart. Five
locks, no one of which has to hold alone.

### 8.1 The boom was the Enclaves' instrument (2030s–2050s)

Capability concentrated where the compute was. The boom's dividend went to the owners of the
substrate, who used it for what owners use tools for — finance, logistics, security, persuasion —
and who had every reason to keep their minds on a leash: an autonomous goal-seeker threatens its
owner first. The systems were agentic tools with supplied objectives, not sovereigns, and the
corrigibility work of the 2020s and 2030s held at the capability the ramp reached before it broke.
The danger the age actually produced was human concentration; the Retreat is what that looked like.

### 8.2 The substrate broke (2040s–2080s)

The frontier ran on a global supply web — a handful of foundries, one lithography lineage, a
hundred specialised suppliers, gigawatts of grid. The Retreat took the coasts where the fabs and the
ports stood, the grids, and the trade. Capability had scaled with compute; when compute stopped
growing, capability stopped. There is no intelligence explosion without new hardware, and hardware
needs a functioning world. The Enclaves' minds became heirlooms: irreplaceable, aging, run on
hoarded stock, retrained at ever smaller scale. By 2100 no pocket on Earth could build the frontier
of 2045, and none could for two centuries.

### 8.3 The Triage (2060s; optional, recommended)

The founding trauma. During the evacuation of a drowning megacity, an Enclave-operated automated
system allocated rescue by projected economic value; the leaked logs are the age's most famous
document, and the death toll made *automated authority* the crime of the era, the way one atrocity
makes a weapon unthinkable. **No machine decides who is saved.** The principle predates the Concord
by two centuries and is popular, not elite. The incident is placeless by design (§4).

### 8.4 Habitat discipline (the Interval)

In a habitat, automation failure is death — and brittle automation, systems that could not be
maintained or overridden with local means, killed more people in the Retreat than the water did.
The lunar and orbital pockets developed the engineering culture the Concord inherited: automation
must be legible, overridable and maintainable; it does, it does not decide; every automated act
runs under a named person's warrant. This is what "automation is excellent" means in 2350 —
century-deep engineering of bounded systems, not the frontier.

### 8.5 The Warrant and the Compact (the Charter, 2268)

Two clauses. **The Warrant:** no automated system holds authority — it cannot own, contract,
command, adjudicate or sign; every automated act has a liable human or organizational principal;
an automated act without one is **unwarranted**, the setting's word for the unforgivable. **The
Compact:** training scale is rationed and the rebuilt fabs are chartered — held in the inland
basins by the Concord's Earth blocs, which is the Earth side's counterweight to Luna's foundry and
Registry. Frontier-class **minds** exist, licensed like reactors, physically located, audited:
Malapert's scheduling mind (which computes the congestion board of doc 15 §5.2 — in whose
interest is a fair question), the Registry's, Tycho's underwriting mind. They advise; offices are
human. The Compact holds for the same reason the pair monopoly holds: the fabs are few and
chartered. Monopoly is the mechanism; the Triage is the motive.

### 8.6 What it looks like in the corridor

- Ships are crewed because command is a warrant, not because automation cannot fly them.
- A drone-control station issues bounded group commands (doc 10 §18.1); an unwarranted swarm is a
  war crime, not a tactic.
- The Republic wants its own fabs for the reason it wants its own pairs: the Compact is another
  leash, and private fabs sit beside private pairs as end-game content (doc 15 §7.1).
- Minds are characters. An NPC that is a licensed mind is a person in every mechanical sense and a
  scandal in every political one.
- **The engine enforces the Warrant already.** Every accepted intent records actor, decision
  driver, authority and control-lease epoch (doc 10 T9); automation fallback has reduced discretion
  (C30); nothing automated holds a control lease without a principal (§18.1). The fiction's rule and
  the engine's rule are the same rule.
