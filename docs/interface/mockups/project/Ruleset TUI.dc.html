Turn 2 — ruleset screens, Console direction

TacSim and Shadowrun in the TUI

Five screens in the Console vocabulary, at 160×44 (the size a HUD like this wants; every pane degrades out on narrower terminals the way the current TUI already does). TacSim panes are addressed in the vocabulary mush-tactics actually has — sections, facets, scalar cells, components, capabilities, mobility modes — so the HUD is a rendering of TacticalUnit rather than a new model. Shadowrun reads the real SR5 sheet fields (condition monitors, limits, Edge, initiative) and adds the two subsystems the ruleset doesn't have yet.

Unit designations, corps, callsigns and hardware names are invented for these mockups — the layouts recreate the functions a THUD-style HUD served, not any published game's material.

T2

TacSim · comms

IC nets left, OOC right. Never the same colour, never the same border.

The one mistake that ruins a tactical scene is saying an IC thing on an OOC channel or vice versa. So IC and OOC are separated structurally, not just prefixed: two panes, cyan versus magenta borders, and a transmit bar whose active net is echoed in the input frame itself. IC traffic carries signal strength and gets degraded text at low signal; OOC never does.

S1

Shadowrun · rigger, jumped in

Two bodies at once — and the screen never lets you forget which one you're in.

A jumped-in rigger's whole problem is that damage arrives in two places and only one of them is where they're looking. So the drone you're piloting owns the big frame in the middle, and your meat body sits in its own permanently-visible pane on the right, with the biofeedback arrow between them. The drone roster keeps every other unit's mode and condition on screen, because a rigger loses drones by forgetting they exist.

S2

Shadowrun · hacker, inside a host

One number dominates the screen, because one number ends the run.

The Matrix has no map, so the spatial pane a rigger gets is replaced by an icon list — what's in here with you, what marks you hold on it, what marks it holds on you. Overwatch is given the loudest element on screen and its own full-width bar, since convergence is the clock the whole run is played against. Matrix attributes sit where the weapon list sits in TacSim, because reallocating them is the loadout decision.

Rules that hold all five screens together
One dominant number per screen. Heat in TacSim, Overwatch in the Matrix, the drone's condition monitor for a rigger. Whatever kills you gets the biggest type and its own bar.
Border colour carries meaning, not decoration. Amber = your unit and your input. Cyan = IC comms. Magenta = OOC and the Matrix. Red = the thing currently hurting you. It's the same four colours on every screen.
Gauges everywhere, numbers beside them. ▰▱ for the glance, the digits for the decision. Never one without the other.
Two bodies get two panes. A rigger's meat and a hacker's deck are never folded into the thing they're driving — that's how players lose characters they didn't know were dying.
The legend is always on screen. Bottom line, every screen, no exceptions. It's how a returning player relearns the HUD in ten seconds instead of reading help.
What exists vs. what these assume
Already there. TacticalUnit sections, facets, scalar cells, components, conditions, capabilities, MobilityProfile modes, the Journal, the hex MapBlob and the tactics.map panel feed with keyframe + delta frames. T1's section pane and all of T3 are renderings of data the crate already produces.
Already there. SR5's two condition monitors, wound modifier, limits, Edge/edge_current, initiative base and dice, and the matrix domain tag in the NPC agent. Every meat-body and limits pane above reads existing fields.
New panel feeds needed. tactics.contacts, tactics.weapons (cycle timers as cells + a ready flag), comms.nets (IC nets with signal strength, separate from comsys), sr.rig, sr.matrix.
New subsystems. There is no Matrix or rigging module yet — S1 and S2 are a design target, not a skin over existing state. The good news is the shapes they need (bounded cells, opaque conditions, capability bits) are exactly what mush-tactics already models, so a drone and a deck can both be TacticalUnits.
Line mode still works. Every pane here has a text fallback: contacts and weapons are already tables, the section pane is the labelled-gutter form, and Overwatch is one line. Nothing in T1–S2 requires cursor addressing to be playable.
